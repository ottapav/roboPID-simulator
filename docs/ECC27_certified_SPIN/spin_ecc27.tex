\begin{abstract}
Model-free PID tuners improve a loop from closed-loop data but do not
guarantee its robustness, and checking the sensitivity peak $M_s$ of a
running loop usually needs a model or dedicated experiments. We give a
deterministic certificate of $M_s$ from one ordinary setpoint step, the
experiment such tuners already perform. In a stable digital loop, the
increments of the error after a setpoint step form the impulse response
of the sensitivity function; from a finite record we derive computable
lower and upper bounds on $M_s$, with explicit tail, grid and
high-frequency terms and one assumption on the unrecorded tail. As an
acceptance test, the certificate lets any iterative tuner that applies
setpoint steps accept only tunings with $M_s\le M^*$, a uniform back-off
of the gains always restores acceptance, and the same record certifies
stability and $M_s$ of a proposed tuning before it is applied. We work
this out for \spin, which counts the turns of the control error in phase
portraits: the counts bound $M_s$ only on special loops, while certified
\spin provably ends below $M^*$ and safe \spin never applies a tuning
above it. On $19$ loops, published \spin ended above $M^*=1.7$ on up to
$9$ and applied tunings above it in $57\%$ of its iterations; safe
\spin, in none.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Let $L=CP$ be the loop transfer function and $S=1/(1+L)$ the sensitivity
function. Its peak $M_s=\max_\omega|S|$ measures robustness: near $1.4$
is safe, near $2$ is poor \cite{AstromHagglund2004}. Model-based tuning
rules reach a chosen $M_s$ \cite{AstromHagglund2004,Skogestad2003}.
Model-free tuners adjust the gains from closed-loop data instead, by
iterative feedback tuning \cite{IFT1998}, extremum seeking
\cite{Killingsworth2006}, virtual reference feedback tuning \cite{VRFT2002},
setpoint overshoot \cite{Shamsuzzoha2010} or step-response inspection
\cite{SPIN}; they target performance or a calibrated response shape, and
none guarantees the $M_s$ of its result. Once a loop runs, its $M_s$ is
rarely checked at all \cite{Desborough2002,Bauer2016}.

The $\mathcal H_\infty$ norm of a system, and hence $M_s$, can be
estimated from data: by power iterations over repeated dedicated
experiments \cite{Wahlberg2010,Rojas2012}, by data-driven estimation
\cite{vanHeusden2007}, from limit cycles induced in closed loop
\cite{Garcia2003}, or from impulse-response data under a decay
assumption, with a one-sided guarantee \cite{Tu2017}; margins can be
bounded from data \cite{Isoshima2023} or measured by relay tests and
closed-loop identification \cite{WangHangBi1997,VandenHofSchrama1993}.
These methods need dedicated experiments, give estimates or one-sided
bounds, and are not built into a tuner. Safe Bayesian optimization tunes
controllers without leaving a safe set \cite{Berkenkamp2016}, with
probabilistic guarantees under a Gaussian-process model of the cost.

We use the experiment a tuner already performs: a setpoint step. That
the error increments after the step are the impulse response of $S$ is
elementary (Lemma~\ref{lem:record}). The contributions are what it
enables:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a deterministic, two-sided certificate of $M_s$ computable from
one finite record (Section~\ref{sec:cert}, Theorem~\ref{thm:cert});
\item for any iterative model-free tuner, a certified acceptance test
(Theorem~\ref{thm:spin}) and a certificate of stability and $M_s$ of a
proposed tuning \emph{before} it is applied (Theorem~\ref{thm:ahead}),
which makes the tuning safe throughout, not only at its end
(Section~\ref{sec:accept});
\item their application to \spin \cite{SPIN}: why the turn counts of
\spin cannot certify (Lemma~\ref{lem:half}, Proposition~\ref{prop:exact}),
certified \spin, which ends below $M^*$, and safe \spin, which never
applies a tuning above it (Section~\ref{sec:app},
Corollaries~\ref{cor:spin} and~\ref{cor:safe}).
\end{enumerate}
Section~\ref{sec:sim} confirms the results in simulation.

\section{A Certificate from One Setpoint Step}
\label{sec:cert}

A digital controller runs with the sampling period $T_s$. Seen at the
samples, the loop it closes is linear and time invariant. Throughout,
$L(z)$, $P(z)$, $S=1/(1+L)$ and
$M_s=\max_{\theta\in[0,\pi]}|S(e^{j\theta})|$, with $\theta=\omega T_s$,
refer to this digital loop, the loop that actually runs.

\begin{lemma}[The step contains $S$]
\label{lem:record}
Let the loop be stable, have one degree of freedom, and have a strictly
proper $L$ (the measurement precedes the control action). Let $e_n$ be
the error after a unit setpoint step at $n=0$, applied from rest and
without disturbances, so that $e_{-1}=0$ and $e_0=1$, and let
$a_n=e_n-e_{n-1}$. Then
\begin{equation}
S(e^{j\theta})=\sum_{n\ge0}a_n\,e^{-jn\theta}.
\label{eq:srecord}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The $z$-transform of $e$ is $S(z)/(1-z^{-1})$, so
$S=(1-z^{-1})\mathcal Z\{e\}$. In a stable loop, $a_n$ decays
exponentially, so the series converges on the unit circle.
\end{proof}

A real record is finite, so one assumption covers what it does not show.

\begin{assumption}[Long enough record]
\label{ass:tail}
The loop is stable, and the record $e_0,\dots,e_N$ satisfies
$\sum_{n>N}|a_n|\le\tau$ for a known $\tau$.
\end{assumption}

In a stable loop, $|a_n|\le c\rho^n$ with $\rho<1$, so $\tau$ falls
geometrically with the record length.

\begin{theorem}[Certified bounds from one record]
\label{thm:cert}
Let the conditions of Lemma~\ref{lem:record} and
Assumption~\ref{ass:tail} hold, and let
$\hat S(\theta)=\sum_{n=0}^{N}a_ne^{-jn\theta}$. Let $G$ be a uniform
grid on $[0,\bar\theta]$, $\bar\theta\le\pi$, that has spacing $h$ and
contains both ends. For any $n_c$, let
\begin{align*}
J&=\textstyle\sum_{n=0}^N|n-n_c|\,|a_n|,\;\;
B=\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}|a_{n+1}-a_n|+|a_N|+2\tau.
\end{align*}
Then $\underline M\le M_s\le\overline M$, where
\begin{align}
\underline M&=\textstyle\max_{\theta\in G}|\hat S(\theta)|-\tau,\nonumber\\
\overline M&=\max\Big\{\textstyle\max_{\theta\in G}|\hat S(\theta)|+\frac{hJ}{2}+\tau,\;
1+\frac{B}{\sin(\bar\theta/2)}\Big\},
\label{eq:bound}
\end{align}
and the second term is omitted if $\bar\theta=\pi$. If $\tau$ equals the
true remaining variation, $N\to\infty$, $h\to0$ and $\bar\theta=\pi$,
then both bounds converge to $M_s$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:srecord}, $|S-\hat S|\le\sum_{n>N}|a_n|\le\tau$ at every
$\theta$, which gives $\underline M$. On $[0,\bar\theta]$, $|\hat S|$
equals $|e^{jn_c\theta}\hat S|$, whose derivative is bounded by $J$.
Every $\theta$ lies within $h/2$ of a grid point, which gives the first
term of $\overline M$. On $[\bar\theta,\pi]$, let $z=e^{-j\theta}$ and
$Z_n=\sum_{m=1}^nz^m$, so that $|Z_n|\le2/|1-z|=1/\sin(\theta/2)$.
Summation by parts gives $\sum_{n\ge1}a_nz^n=\sum_{n\ge1}(a_n-a_{n+1})Z_n$.
Since $\sum_{n\ge N}|a_n-a_{n+1}|\le|a_N|+2\tau$, we obtain
$|S-a_0|\le B/\sin(\theta/2)$ with $a_0=1$. Convergence follows because,
for fixed $n_c$, $J$ stays bounded as $N\to\infty$, so $hJ\to0$, and the
remaining variation tends to zero.
\end{proof}

Everything in \eqref{eq:bound} except $\tau$ comes from the record, and
all grid values follow from one chirp $z$-transform. The grid term
covers the frequencies up to $\bar\theta$; above them, $B$ bounds $|S|$
at once, and it is small for a smooth record. Fig.~\ref{fig:record}
shows both steps on one loop.

\section{A Certified Acceptance Test}
\label{sec:accept}

An iterative tuner applies a controller $C$, records a setpoint step, and
proposes the next controller. \emph{Certified acceptance} keeps the
tuner's own rule for proposing, but accepts $C$ only if the upper bound
$\overline M$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}, computed from the record of $C$,
is at most a target $M^*$; otherwise it lowers the gains, by the tuner's
own rule or by a uniform back-off $C\to(1-\beta)C$. Here $C$ has integral
action, $C=C_0+K_iT_s z^{-1}/(1-z^{-1})$ with $C_0$ stable,
and $\bar\theta$ in \eqref{eq:bound} solves
$1+B/\sin(\bar\theta/2)=M^*$ ($\bar\theta=\pi$ if no solution), with a
grid fine enough that $hJ/2\le\eta$.

\begin{theorem}[Certified acceptance]
\label{thm:spin}
Let the plant be stable, let $K_iP(1)>0$, and let every tested record
meet the conditions of Lemma~\ref{lem:record} and
Assumption~\ref{ass:tail}.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] Every accepted tuning has $M_s\le M^*$, whatever rule
proposed it.
\item[(ii)] For a controller $C$, let
$\mu=\min\{0,\inf_{0<\theta\le\pi}\mathrm{Re}\,L(e^{j\theta})\}$. For
$0<\lambda<1/|\mu|$, the loop with $\lambda C$ is stable, and
\begin{equation}
M_s(\lambda C)\le\frac{1}{1-\lambda|\mu|}.
\label{eq:backoff}
\end{equation}
\item[(iii)] If $M^*>1+2\tau+\eta$, then from any such $C$ at most
$j^*=\lceil\ln\frac{|\mu|M'}{M'-1}/\ln\frac1{1-\beta}\rceil$ uniform
back-offs lead to an accepted tuning, where $M'=M^*-2\tau-\eta$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) This follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}. (ii) $L$ is bounded on the
unit circle except near $z=1$, where the integral term has a bounded real
part, because $P$ is analytic at $z=1$ and
$\mathrm{Re}\,1/(e^{j\theta}-1)=-\frac12$; so $\mu$ is finite. Then
$|1+\lambda L|\ge1+\lambda\,\mathrm{Re}\,L\ge1-\lambda|\mu|>0$, which
gives \eqref{eq:backoff}. The Nyquist curve of $\lambda L$ lies in
$\mathrm{Re}\ge-\lambda|\mu|>-1$, also on a small indentation outside
$z=1$, where $K_iP(1)>0$ keeps $\mathrm{Re}\,L>\mu$; it cannot encircle
$-1$, and $L$ has no poles outside the unit disc, so the loop is stable.
(iii) After $j$ back-offs, $\lambda=(1-\beta)^j$, and \eqref{eq:backoff}
gives $M_s\le M'$ for $j\ge j^*$. In \eqref{eq:bound}, the grid term is
then at most $M_s+2\tau+\eta\le M^*$, and the second term is at most
$M^*$ by the choice of $\bar\theta$; so the test passes.
\end{proof}

Acceptance tests a tuning after it has been applied. The same record
also certifies any other controller \emph{before} it is applied, so a
tuner can avoid applying a fragile tuning at all.

\begin{theorem}[Certify before applying]
\label{thm:ahead}
Let the record of $C$ meet the conditions of Lemma~\ref{lem:record} and
Assumption~\ref{ass:tail}, with $C$ as in Theorem~\ref{thm:spin}, and
let $C'$ be such a controller with the same $T_s$, with $K_i'>0$ and
$\Delta=C'/C-1$ bounded on the unit circle. Let $\hat T=1-\hat S$. Then
$S'=S/(1+T\Delta)$ exactly, with $T=1-S$, and if, for all
$\theta\in(0,\pi]$,
\begin{equation}
d(\theta)=\min_{0\le\lambda\le1}|1+\lambda\hat T\Delta|-\tau|\Delta|>0,
\label{eq:ahead}
\end{equation}
then $C'$ stabilizes the plant, and
\begin{equation}
M_s(C')\le\sup_{0<\theta\le\pi}\frac{|\hat S|+\tau}{|1+\hat T\Delta|-\tau|\Delta|}.
\label{eq:aheadbound}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$L'=L(1+\Delta)$ gives $1+L'=(1+L)(1+T\Delta)$. Let
$C_\lambda=C(1+\lambda\Delta)=(1-\lambda)C+\lambda C'$, $\lambda\in[0,1]$.
Its integral gain is positive and its other poles are those of $C_0$
and $C_0'$, inside the unit disc, so $L_\lambda$ has no poles outside the unit disc and is
large on a small indentation around $z=1$. Since
$|T-\hat T|=|S-\hat S|\le\tau$, $|1+\lambda T\Delta|\ge d>0$, so
$1+L_\lambda\neq0$ on the indented circle for every $\lambda$. The
winding number of $1+L_\lambda$ about $0$ is thus constant in $\lambda$,
and at $\lambda=0$ the loop is stable. The bound follows from $|S|\le|\hat S|+\tau$ and
$|1+T\Delta|\ge|1+\hat T\Delta|-\tau|\Delta|$.
\end{proof}

Like \eqref{eq:bound}, \eqref{eq:ahead} and \eqref{eq:aheadbound} are
checked on a grid with Lipschitz margins; above $\bar\theta$, the bound
$|S-1|\le r=B/\sin(\theta/2)$ from the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}
replaces the record, and \eqref{eq:ahead} holds if $r|\Delta|<1$, with
$|S'|\le(1+r)/(1-r|\Delta|)$. As $\tau\to0$, \eqref{eq:aheadbound}
becomes the exact prediction $\max|\hat S/(1+\hat T\Delta)|$, and a
back-off is the case $\Delta=\lambda-1$.

\section{Application to \spin}
\label{sec:app}

\subsection{The \spin method}

\spin \cite{SPIN} tunes an I, PI or PID controller with the gains
$K_i,K_p,K_d$. In each iteration, it applies one setpoint step, records
the error $e$, and forms three phase portraits,
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_0:(e,\textstyle\sum e),\quad \Gamma_1:(\Delta e,e),\quad
\Gamma_2:(\Delta^2e,\Delta e),
\label{eq:portraits}
\end{equation}
where $\sum e$ is the running sum of $e$ and $\Delta$ the difference of
consecutive samples. They correspond to the frequency bands of $K_i$,
$K_p$ and $K_d$, which we call bands $0$, $1$ and $2$. Each axis is
divided by its peak, and the count $N_k$ is the number of turns that
$\Gamma_k$ makes around its end point before the error stays within
$\pm\delta$ ($2\%$ of the step). Algorithm~1 states the rule.\footnote{An
online simulator: \url{https://robopid-simulator.uceeb.cvut.cz}}

\subsection{Why the turn counts cannot certify}
\label{sec:count}

In each portrait \eqref{eq:portraits}, the second coordinate $q$ is the
running sum of the first coordinate $p$. The count is the net
counterclockwise angle around the end point, placed at the origin,
divided by $2\pi$; we state the results in continuous time, where
$\dot q=p$. For $\Gamma_0$, the zeros of $p=e$ are the setpoint
crossings.

\begin{lemma}[Half turns]
\label{lem:half}
Let $\dot q=p$ with $p$ continuous, let $p(t_1)=p(t_2)=0$ with
$q(t_1)q(t_2)<0$, and let $p$ not change sign on $(t_1,t_2)$. Then, on
$[t_1,t_2]$, the curve $(p,q)$ turns counterclockwise around the origin
by exactly half a turn, and so does its image under any linear map with
a positive determinant, such as the per-axis normalization of \spin.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On $(t_1,t_2)$, $p$ keeps one sign, so the curve stays in one closed
half-plane bounded by the $q$-axis, and $q$ moves monotonically from one
ray of the $q$-axis to the opposite ray; it avoids the origin, since
$q=0$ only where $p\neq0$. The angle thus changes by exactly $\pi$, and
counterclockwise, since $q$ increases where $p>0$ and decreases where
$p<0$. A linear map with a positive determinant maps half-planes to
half-planes and opposite rays to opposite rays, and keeps the
orientation.
\end{proof}

So each setpoint crossing adds half a turn to $N_0$, and how many
crossings a count sees depends on how many rise above $\delta$. Whether
crossings reveal $M_s$ depends on the loop. Lambda, IMC and SIMC tunings
cancel the plant lags \cite{Skogestad2003}, e.g., PI with $T_i=T$ on
$Ke^{-sL_d}/(Ts+1)$; the loop is then
\begin{equation}
L(s)=\frac{k\,e^{-sL_d}}{sL_d},\qquad k=\frac{KK_cL_d}{T_i}.
\label{eq:loop}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Crossings on lag-cancelled loops]
\label{prop:exact}
The loop \eqref{eq:loop} is stable for $0<k<\pi/2$, and its $M_s$
strictly increases with $k$, as given by
\begin{equation}
k=x\sin x-x^2\cos x,\qquad
\frac{1}{M_s^2}=1-\frac{2k\sin x}{x}+\frac{k^2}{x^2},
\label{eq:param}
\end{equation}
with $x\in(0,\pi/2)$ the normalized peak frequency. The error after a
setpoint step never crosses the setpoint if and only if
$k\le1/\mathrm{e}$, that is, $M_s\le1.394$; otherwise it crosses
infinitely often.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The phase of $L$ is $-\pi/2-x$ at $x=\omega L_d$; it reaches $-\pi$ first
at $x=\pi/2$, where $|L|=2k/\pi<1$, and $|L|=k/x$ decreases, so the loop
is stable. Let $h(x)=|1+L|^2=1-2k\sin x/x+k^2/x^2$. Then
$h'(x)=2k(\varphi(x)-k)/x^3$ with $\varphi(x)=x\sin x-x^2\cos x$
increasing on $(0,\pi)$ and $\varphi(\pi/2)>k$, so $h$ has one minimum
$x^*\in(0,\pi/2)$ on $(0,\pi)$; on $[\pi,2\pi]$, $h\ge1$, and for
$x\ge2\pi$, $h\ge(1-k/x)^2>h(\pi/2)$. This gives \eqref{eq:param}, and by
the envelope theorem $\frac{d}{dk}h(x^*)=-2\cos x^*<0$. The error obeys
$\dot e(t)=-(k/L_d)e(t-L_d)$ with $e=1$ on $[0,L_d]$; it stays positive
if and only if the characteristic equation $s+(k/L_d)e^{-sL_d}=0$ has a
real root, that is, if $k\le1/\mathrm{e}$, and otherwise all solutions
oscillate \cite{GyoriLadas1991}.
\end{proof}

At $\delta=0.02$ the counts of the loop \eqref{eq:loop} therefore form a
staircase in $M_s$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:stair}(a)). With $n$ crossings above
$\delta$, the computed $N_0$ and $N_1$ lie within a quarter turn above
$n/2$, and $N_2$ is $n/2-1/4$ within $0.01$: on this ideal loop $\dot e$
jumps at $t=L_d$, so $\Gamma_2$ starts with a quarter turn clockwise. The published limits lie in
the gaps: $\bar N_0=1/2$, $\bar N_1=3/4$ and $\bar N_2=1$ allow $0$, $1$
and $2$ crossings above $\delta$, that is, $M_s\le1.534$, $1.842$ and
$2.210$, where the $(n+1)$th extremum equals $\delta$. Each band allows
one crossing more than the band below, which is why the limits grow;
$\Gamma_0$ binds, so on these loops \spin stops at $M_s\le1.534$.

On general loops, this guarantee is lost. The counts leave the
staircase: in the $3800$ count decisions of Section~\ref{sec:sim}, $N_0$
and $N_2$ fell between $n/2+1/4$ and $(n+1)/2$ in $22$ and $67\%$ of
them. And crossings do not bound $M_s$: a first-order plant with
$T=0.3L_d$ under PI with $T_i=8T$ and a gain margin of $1.624$ has
$M_s=2.62$, yet its error never crosses the setpoint by more than $2\%$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:record}(a)), while a controller zero can cause crossings
at a low $M_s$. The counts can shape a tuning, but only a certificate can
certify it.

\subsection{Certified \spin}
\label{sec:spin}

\emph{Certified \spin accepts a tuning that the counts accept only if the
upper bound $\overline M$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}, computed from the
same record, is at most a target $M^*$.} In Algorithm~1, it replaces
line 7 by the bold lines 7a--7c: the counts shape the tuning, and the
certificate decides whether it is accepted. A rejected tuning is lowered
by the band move that the prediction of Theorem~\ref{thm:ahead} expects
to lower $M_s$, as in line 10, or by a uniform back-off, the reverse of
an acceptance.

\begin{corollary}[Certified \spin]
\label{cor:spin}
Under the conditions of Theorem~\ref{thm:spin}, the result of certified
\spin has $M_s\le M^*$, whatever the limits $\bar N_k$. If no gain is
clipped by the gain box, every run of rejections in line 7b ends in an
acceptance or in a move of Algorithm~1 (line 3 or 10), after at most
$n_{\max}$ band moves and at most $j^*$ back-offs in each stretch of
back-offs.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Line 7c allows at most $n_{\max}$ band moves in a row; the rest follows
from Theorem~\ref{thm:spin}(i) and (iii), unless the counts reject first.
\end{proof}

So the test never traps \spin. It does not stop \spin from
\emph{applying} fragile tunings: each is tested before it is rejected.
\emph{Safe \spin} certifies every move before it is applied. It applies
the move of Algorithm~1 only if Theorem~\ref{thm:ahead} certifies it
with a bound \eqref{eq:aheadbound} at most $M^*$; otherwise the first so
certified of the band moves $k=2,1,0$ and the uniform back-off; otherwise
it keeps the current tuning. Until line 7b first accepts, it backs off
uniformly; a tuning certified ahead that line 7b does not accept is
replaced by the last accepted one.

\begin{corollary}[Safe \spin]
\label{cor:safe}
Under the conditions of Theorem~\ref{thm:spin}, from the first accepted
tuning on, every tuning that safe \spin applies stabilizes the plant and
has $M_s\le M^*$, whatever the limits $\bar N_k$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Moves are proposed only from accepted tunings, so each applied tuning
is certified by Theorem~\ref{thm:ahead} from the record of an accepted
tuning, or was accepted itself (Theorem~\ref{thm:spin}(i)).
\end{proof}

\section{Simulations}
\label{sec:sim}

\paragraph*{Setup} We compare published and certified \spin. Both run
$200$ iterations with a derivative filter ratio of $10$ and the gain box
of \cite{SPIN}. Since the box ties the result to the start, every loop
runs from SIMC on a half-rule model with $\tau_c$ three times its dead
time (cautious start) and from the same tuning with all gains doubled
(bold start). The $19$ test loops are (1) the four plants of
\cite{SPIN}, $P_1=e^{-s}/((10s+1)(s+1)^3)$, $P_2=1.25e^{-8s}/(5s+1)^4$,
$P_3=e^{-10s}/((2s+1)(s+1)^3)$ and $P_4=e^{-4s}/(8s+1)^6$, with PI and
with PID; (2) four plants that place the oscillation in a chosen band:
the delay-dominant $e^{-5s}/(s+1)$ and the lag-dominant $e^{-s}/(10s+1)$
and $e^{-0.5s}/((10s+1)(s+1))$ with PI, and the fast
$e^{-0.1s}/((s+1)(0.5s+1))$ with PID; (3) the family
$e^{-L_ds}/((s+1)(0.5s+1)(0.25s+1))$ with PID and
$L_d\in\{0.25,0.5,1,\dots,16\}$ (dead-time share $0.125$ to $0.90$).
P, I and D act on the error. Let $T_a$ be the dead time plus the lag
time constants and $T_f$ the derivative filter time constant; the
controller runs at $T_s=\min(T_a/100,T_f/20)$ for PID and $T_a/1000$ for
PI, with exactly discretized lags and the delay rounded to whole samples.
Each record covers $15\,T_a$. All values of $M_s$ are those of this
digital loop, computed from its exact $L$. Because \spin ends in a cycle
between a few tunings, we report the most aggressive tuning accepted in
the last $60$ iterations.

\paragraph*{The certificate (Section~\ref{sec:cert})} For all $146$
tunings accepted below, a four times longer record showed a remaining
variation $\le2.8\cdot10^{-5}$, so Assumption~\ref{ass:tail} held with
$\tau=10^{-3}$; \eqref{eq:srecord} held up to this variation, and every
$M_s$ lay within its bounds, with $\overline M-M_s\le\eta+\tau+2.8\cdot
10^{-5}$. Short records give wider bounds that still contain $M_s$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:record}(b)).
% confirm_thm1.py, fig_record.py

\paragraph*{The counts (Section~\ref{sec:count})} On the lag-cancelled
loop, $\Gamma_0$ turned by half a turn between crossings within
$10^{-6}$, also after a linear map, and \eqref{eq:param} matched a
direct search for $M_s$ to six digits.
% check_result1.py, confirm_limits.py

\paragraph*{Certified \spin (Section~\ref{sec:spin}; Table~\ref{tab:spin}, Fig.~\ref{fig:certified})}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Safety.} Published \spin ended above $M^*=1.7$ on $9$ of the
$19$ loops from the cautious start (up to $2.01$), on $8$ from the bold
start (up to $2.09$) and, without the gain box, on $12$ of $18$ (up to
$2.05$); certified \spin on none (at most $1.69$), as
Theorem~\ref{thm:spin}(i) guarantees.
\item \emph{No trapping.} \eqref{eq:backoff} held along each back-off,
predicted and true $M_s$ after every band move agreed within $10^{-7}$,
and runs of rejections had at most $4$ band moves; every loop kept
accepting, except $L_d=16$ without the box, where the counts lower $K_d$
every round under either rule.
\item \emph{Cost.} The load-step IAE rose by $7.8\%$ on average ($6.4\%$
from the bold start).
\end{itemize}
% designed.py (cert-shape), confirm_thm2.py, analyze_cert.py

\paragraph*{Safety during tuning (Corollary~\ref{cor:safe}, Fig.~\ref{fig:certified}(c))}
Theorem~\ref{thm:ahead} needs one $T_s$, so we reran all three variants
with $T_s$ fixed at its value for the start tuning (published \spin then
ended above $M^*$ on $9$ and $7$ loops). In the $7600$ iterations of the
$38$ runs, published \spin applied a tuning with $M_s>M^*$ in $4371$
(up to $M_s=2.38$), certified \spin, which must apply a tuning to reject
it, in $3398$ (up to $1.98$), and safe \spin in none: all its tunings
were stable, with $M_s\le1.696$. Every start tuning was accepted. Safe
\spin ended with $M_s\le1.694$ and the same median load-step IAE as
certified \spin, but up to twice as large on the fast PID loop. On $456$
random moves (gains scaled by up to $8$) from accepted tunings,
\eqref{eq:ahead} held for $339$, all stable, and failed for $117$, all
unstable; \eqref{eq:aheadbound} exceeded $M_s$ by a median of $0.011$
and certified $116$ of the $118$ moves with $M_s\le M^*$.
% run_safe.sh, analyze_safe.py, confirm_thm3.py

\paragraph*{Limit values under the certificate} With every limit $1/2$,
certified \spin still stayed below $M^*$ (at most $1.68$), but the counts
did the rejecting (the certificate $14$ and $8$ times in the last $60$
iterations, against $151$ and $172$): $2$ and $3$ loops accepted no
tuning, and the load-step IAE rose by a median of $11$ and $16\%$, up to
sixteenfold (fast PID, $M_s=1.21$). Raising $\bar N_1$ to $1$ changed
nothing, since $N_1$ never lay in $(3/4,1]$. Under the certificate, the
limits set performance, not robustness.
% run_limits.sh, analyze_limits.py

\section{Conclusion}

The setpoint step that a model-free tuner applies anyway certifies the
robustness of the loop it tests: one finite record gives two-sided bounds
on $M_s$ without a model and without a dedicated experiment, and so does
every logged setpoint change made from rest. As an acceptance test, the
certificate makes any iterative tuner accept only tunings with $M_s\le
M^*$, a uniform back-off always restores acceptance, and certifying each
move before it is applied keeps every applied tuning below $M^*$. For \spin, the
turn counts measure setpoint crossings and bound $M_s$ only on special
loops; with the certificate they keep shaping the tuning, and the
certificate decides what is accepted. The theorems assume a linear loop
and a long enough record; noise and real loops are left to a follow-up.
Scripts: \url{https://github.com/ottapav/robopid-simulator}.
