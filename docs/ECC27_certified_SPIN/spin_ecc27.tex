\begin{abstract}
Model-free PID tuners improve a loop from closed-loop data, but none
guarantees its robustness, measured by the peak $M_s$ of the sensitivity
function. We show that the setpoint step these tuners already apply is
enough to certify it. In a stable digital loop, the increments of the
error after a unit setpoint step are the impulse response of the
sensitivity function. One finite record therefore gives a lower and an
upper bound on $M_s$, under one assumption on the unrecorded rest of the
response. The same record also certifies the stability and the $M_s$ of
any other controller before it is applied. With these two certificates,
any iterative tuner can accept only tunings with $M_s\le M^*$, and never
apply a tuning with $M_s>M^*$. We add them to \spin, a tuner that counts
the turns of the error in phase portraits, in two steps: certified \spin
checks each tuning after applying it, safe \spin before. On $19$
simulated loops with $M^*=1.7$, basic \spin ended above $M^*$ on up to
$9$ loops and applied tunings above $M^*$ in $57\%$ of its iterations;
certified \spin ended below $M^*$ on all loops, but still applied such
tunings in $45\%$; safe \spin never applied one.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Let $C$ be the controller, $P$ the plant, $L=CP$ the loop transfer
function, $S=1/(1+L)$ the sensitivity function and $T=1-S$ the
complementary sensitivity function. The peak $M_s=\max_\omega|S|$
measures robustness: $1/M_s$ is the shortest distance from the Nyquist
curve of $L$ to the critical point $-1$, so $M_s$ near $1.4$ is safe and
near $2$ is poor \cite{AstromHagglund2004}. Model-based tuning rules
reach a chosen $M_s$ \cite{AstromHagglund2004,Skogestad2003}. Model-free
tuners adjust the gains from closed-loop data instead, by iterative
feedback tuning \cite{IFT1998}, extremum seeking
\cite{Killingsworth2006}, virtual reference feedback tuning
\cite{VRFT2002}, setpoint overshoot \cite{Shamsuzzoha2010} or
step-response inspection \cite{SPIN}. They target performance or a
response shape; none guarantees the $M_s$ of its result, or of the
tunings it tries on the way. Once a loop runs, its $M_s$ is rarely
checked at all \cite{Desborough2002,Bauer2016}.

The $\mathcal H_\infty$ norm of a system, and hence $M_s$, can be
estimated from data: by power iterations over repeated dedicated
experiments \cite{Wahlberg2010,Rojas2012}, by data-driven estimation
\cite{vanHeusden2007}, from limit cycles induced in closed loop
\cite{Garcia2003}, or from impulse-response data under a decay
assumption, with a one-sided guarantee \cite{Tu2017}; margins can be
bounded from data \cite{Isoshima2023}.
These methods need dedicated experiments, give estimates or one-sided
bounds, and are not built into a tuner. Safe Bayesian optimization tunes
controllers without leaving a safe set \cite{Berkenkamp2016}, with
probabilistic guarantees under a Gaussian-process model of the cost.

\emph{The idea.} A tuner already applies a setpoint step in every
iteration. After a unit step, the increments $a_n=e_n-e_{n-1}$ of the
error $e$ are the impulse response of $S$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:record}), so
their Fourier sum is $S$ itself. One record thus shows $|S|$ at every
frequency, and with it the peak $M_s$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:record}). We turn
this elementary fact into guarantees, for a chosen target $M^*$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{bounds}: one finite record gives a lower and an upper bound
on $M_s$ that hold with certainty, not estimates (Section~\ref{sec:cert},
Theorem~\ref{thm:cert});
\item \emph{tests for any iterative tuner}: a test that passes only
tunings with $M_s\le M^*$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:spin}), and a test of
the next tuning \emph{before} it is applied (Theorem~\ref{thm:ahead}),
which keeps every applied tuning below $M^*$ (Section~\ref{sec:accept});
\item \emph{application to \spin} \cite{SPIN}: two variants built on
the tests, certified \spin, which ends below $M^*$, and safe \spin,
which never applies a tuning above $M^*$ (Section~\ref{sec:app},
Corollaries~\ref{cor:spin} and~\ref{cor:safe}), and why its turn counts
alone cannot certify $M_s$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:half},
Proposition~\ref{prop:exact}).
\end{enumerate}
Section~\ref{sec:sim} confirms the results in simulation.
Table~\ref{tab:symbols} lists the main symbols.

A digital controller runs with the sampling period $T_s$. Seen at the
sampling instants, the loop it closes is linear and time invariant, with
transfer functions $C(z)$, $P(z)$, $L$, $S$ and $T$ as above. On the
unit circle, $z=\mathrm{e}^{j\theta}$ with the normalized frequency
$\theta=\omega T_s\in[0,\pi]$, and
$M_s=\max_{\theta}|S(\mathrm{e}^{j\theta})|$; we write $M_s(C)$ when the
controller matters. All these quantities refer to the digital loop, the
loop that actually runs; $\mathrm{e}$ (upright) is Euler's number.

\begin{lemma}[The step contains $S$]
\label{lem:record}
Let the loop be stable and have one degree of freedom (the controller
acts on the error $e=r-y$, with setpoint $r$ and output $y$), and let $L$
be strictly proper (the measurement precedes the control action). Let
$e_n$ be the error after a unit setpoint step at $n=0$, applied from
rest and without disturbances, so that $e_{-1}=0$ and $e_0=1$, and let
$a_n=e_n-e_{n-1}$. Then
\begin{equation}
S(\mathrm{e}^{j\theta})=\sum_{n\ge0}a_n\,\mathrm{e}^{-jn\theta}.
\label{eq:srecord}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $R(z)$ and $E(z)$ be the $z$-transforms of the setpoint and the
error. A unit step has $R=1/(1-z^{-1})$, and $E=SR$, so
$S=(1-z^{-1})E$, whose coefficients are the increments $a_n$. In a
stable loop, $a_n$ decays exponentially, so the series converges on the
unit circle.
\end{proof}

A real record $e_0,\dots,e_N$ is finite, so one assumption covers the
increments it does not show.

\begin{assumption}[Long enough record]
\label{ass:tail}
The loop is stable, and the record $e_0,\dots,e_N$ satisfies
$\sum_{n>N}|a_n|\le\tau$ for a known $\tau$.
\end{assumption}

This is the only assumption the certificate needs. A valid $\tau$
shrinks geometrically as the record gets longer (Section~\ref{sec:sim}
checks it with a four times longer record). No finite record can prove
that the loop is stable or that the tail is small: an unstable or slow
mode may appear only after the record ends. So Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}
bounds $M_s$ of a loop assumed stable, and Theorem~\ref{thm:ahead} later
certifies the stability of each next controller from the record of the
current one.

By \eqref{eq:srecord}, the record gives the estimate
$\hat S(\theta)=\sum_{n=0}^{N}a_n\mathrm{e}^{-jn\theta}$ of $S$ at every
frequency. Its largest value on a frequency grid differs from $M_s$ for
three reasons, which Theorem~\ref{thm:cert} bounds one by one: the
unrecorded tail (term $\tau$), the gaps between grid points (term
$hJ/2$), and high frequencies, where a grid would have to be very fine
(term $B/\sin(\bar\theta/2)$).

\begin{theorem}[Certified bounds from one record]
\label{thm:cert}
Let Lemma~\ref{lem:record} and Assumption~\ref{ass:tail} hold. Let $G$ be
a uniform grid on $[0,\bar\theta]$, $0<\bar\theta\le\pi$, with spacing
$h$ and containing both ends. For any integer $n_c$, let
\begin{align*}
J&=\textstyle\sum_{n=0}^N|n-n_c|\,|a_n|,\\
B&=\textstyle\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}|a_{n+1}-a_n|+|a_N|+2\tau.
\end{align*}
Then $\underline M\le M_s\le\overline M$, where
\begin{align}
\underline M&=\textstyle\max_{\theta\in G}|\hat S(\theta)|-\tau,\nonumber\\
\overline M&=\max\Big\{\textstyle\max_{\theta\in G}|\hat S(\theta)|+\frac{hJ}{2}+\tau,
\nonumber\\&\qquad\quad 1+\frac{B}{\sin(\bar\theta/2)}\Big\},
\label{eq:bound}
\end{align}
and the second term is omitted if $\bar\theta=\pi$. If $\tau$ equals the
true remaining variation $\sum_{n>N}|a_n|$, then both bounds converge to
$M_s$ as $N\to\infty$ and $h\to0$ with $\bar\theta=\pi$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Tail.} By \eqref{eq:srecord}, $S-\hat S=\sum_{n>N}a_n\mathrm{e}^{-jn\theta}$,
so $|S-\hat S|\le\tau$ at every $\theta$. This gives $\underline M$ and
the term $\tau$ in $\overline M$. \emph{Grid.} On $[0,\bar\theta]$,
$|\hat S(\theta)|=|\sum_na_n\mathrm{e}^{-j(n-n_c)\theta}|$, whose derivative with
respect to $\theta$ is at most $J$ in magnitude. Every $\theta$ lies
within $h/2$ of a grid point, so $|\hat S|$ exceeds its largest grid
value by at most $hJ/2$. \emph{High frequencies.} On
$[\bar\theta,\pi]$, let $z=\mathrm{e}^{-j\theta}$ and $Z_n=\sum_{m=1}^nz^m$, so
that $|Z_n|=|z(1-z^n)/(1-z)|\le2/|1-z|=1/\sin(\theta/2)$. Summation by
parts gives $\sum_{n\ge1}a_nz^n=\sum_{n\ge1}(a_n-a_{n+1})Z_n$, and
$\sum_{n\ge N}|a_n-a_{n+1}|\le|a_N|+2\sum_{n>N}|a_n|\le|a_N|+2\tau$. So
$|S-a_0|\le B/\sin(\theta/2)\le B/\sin(\bar\theta/2)$ with $a_0=1$.
\emph{Convergence.} For a fixed $n_c$, $J$ stays bounded as
$N\to\infty$, so $hJ\to0$, and the remaining variation tends to zero.
\end{proof}

Everything in \eqref{eq:bound} except $\tau$ comes from the record;
$n_c$ only affects tightness (we take the weighted median of $|a_n|$).
All grid values follow from one chirp $z$-transform, an FFT-type
algorithm; the high-frequency term needs no grid at all.
Fig.~\ref{fig:record} shows the bounds on the example that runs
through the paper: plant $P_3$ of Table~\ref{tab:loops} with PID, tuned
by basic \spin.

\section{A Certified Acceptance Test}
\label{sec:accept}

An iterative tuner applies a controller $C$, records the error after a
setpoint step, and proposes the next controller from the record. We call
a set of gains a \emph{tuning}. The \emph{test} adds one check to any
tuner: a tuning \emph{passes} if the upper bound $\overline M$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}, computed from its own record, is at most the
target $M^*$. A tuning that fails is lowered, by the tuner's own rule or
by a \emph{uniform back-off} $C\to(1-\beta)C$, $0<\beta<1$, which scales
all gains down by the same factor. A tuner \emph{accepts} a tuning when
it keeps it as a candidate result; with the test, it accepts only
tunings that pass.

From here on, the plant is stable (so its static gain $P(1)$ is finite),
as are the self-regulating processes that \spin targets, and the
controller has integral action, $C=C_0+K_iT_s/(z-1)$ with $K_i>0$ and a
stable proportional and derivative part $C_0$. So that the test can be
passed, we choose $\bar\theta$ and the grid in \eqref{eq:bound} such
that neither the high-frequency term nor the grid term can push
$\overline M$ above $M^*$ by itself: $1+B/\sin(\bar\theta/2)=M^*$
($\bar\theta=\pi$ if $1+B>M^*$), and $hJ/2\le\eta$ for a small
tolerance $\eta$.

\begin{theorem}[Certified acceptance]
\label{thm:spin}
Let every record meet Lemma~\ref{lem:record} and
Assumption~\ref{ass:tail}. Then every tuning that passes the test has
$M_s\le M^*$, whatever rule proposed it.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Passing means $\overline M\le M^*$, and $M_s\le\overline M$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}.
\end{proof}

So the test never passes a fragile tuning. Nor can it trap a tuner: a
uniform back-off shrinks the Nyquist curve of $L$ towards the origin,
away from $-1$, so it always leads to a tuning that passes.

\begin{proposition}[A back-off restores the test]
\label{prop:backoff}
Let $K_iP(1)>0$ (the integral action counteracts the error), and let
every record meet Lemma~\ref{lem:record} and Assumption~\ref{ass:tail}.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] For a controller $C$, let
$\mu=\min\{0,\inf_{0<\theta\le\pi}\mathrm{Re}\,L(\mathrm{e}^{j\theta})\}$, the
leftmost real part of its Nyquist curve. For $0<\lambda<1/|\mu|$, the
controller $\lambda C$ stabilizes the plant, and
\begin{equation}
M_s(\lambda C)\le\frac{1}{1-\lambda|\mu|}.
\label{eq:backoff}
\end{equation}
\item[(ii)] If $M^*>1+2\tau+\eta$, then at most
$j^*=\lceil\ln\frac{|\mu|M'}{M'-1}/\ln\frac1{1-\beta}\rceil$ uniform
back-offs from $C$ lead to a tuning that passes, where
$M'=M^*-2\tau-\eta$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) First, $\mu$ is finite: $L$ is bounded on
the unit circle except near $z=1$, where the integral term has the real
part $-K_iT_s/2$, since $\mathrm{Re}\,1/(\mathrm{e}^{j\theta}-1)=-\frac12$, and
$P$ is continuous at $z=1$. For every $\theta$,
$|1+\lambda L|\ge1+\lambda\,\mathrm{Re}\,L\ge1-\lambda|\mu|>0$, which
gives \eqref{eq:backoff}. For stability, we apply the Nyquist criterion
on the unit circle with a small indentation that passes outside the pole
$z=1$. On the indentation, $\lambda L\approx\lambda K_iT_sP(1)/(z-1)$ has
a positive real part, since $K_iP(1)>0$. So the whole Nyquist curve of
$\lambda L$ lies in $\mathrm{Re}\ge-\lambda|\mu|>-1$ and cannot encircle
$-1$; as $\lambda L$ has no poles outside the indented circle, the loop
is stable. (ii) After $j$ back-offs, $\lambda=(1-\beta)^j$, and
\eqref{eq:backoff} gives $M_s\le M'$ once $j\ge j^*$. Then the grid term
of \eqref{eq:bound} is at most $(M_s+\tau)+\eta+\tau\le M^*$, and the
high-frequency term is at most $M^*$ by the choice of $\bar\theta$; so
the tuning passes.
\end{proof}

The test checks a tuning only after it has been applied: the
tuner learns that a tuning is fragile only by running it. But the record
of $C$ gives $S$ of the running loop, and $S$ determines the sensitivity
of any other controller $C'$ on the same plant. Let $\rho=C'/C-1$ be the
relative change of the controller, so that $L'=L(1+\rho)$. Then
$1+L'=1+L+L\rho=(1+L)(1+T\rho)$, and
\begin{equation}
S'=\frac{S}{1+T\rho}.
\label{eq:predict}
\end{equation}
This is exact: it predicts $S'$ from $S$ and the planned change $\rho$,
without a model of the plant. The record gives $\hat S$ and
$\hat T=1-\hat S$, each within $\tau$; Theorem~\ref{thm:ahead} accounts
for this error.

\begin{theorem}[Certify before applying]
\label{thm:ahead}
On a stable plant, let $C$ and $C'$ be controllers with integral action,
as in Section~\ref{sec:accept}, and the same $T_s$, let $\rho$ be bounded on the unit circle, and let the record
of $C$ meet Lemma~\ref{lem:record} and Assumption~\ref{ass:tail}. If, for
all $\theta\in(0,\pi]$,
\begin{equation}
d(\theta)=\min_{0\le\lambda\le1}|1+\lambda\hat T\rho|-\tau|\rho|>0,
\label{eq:ahead}
\end{equation}
then $C'$ stabilizes the plant, and
\begin{equation}
M_s(C')\le\sup_{0<\theta\le\pi}\frac{|\hat S|+\tau}{|1+\hat T\rho|-\tau|\rho|}.
\label{eq:aheadbound}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

In words: \eqref{eq:ahead} says that, as the controller moves gradually
from $C$ to $C'$, the Nyquist curve never passes through $-1$; its
minimum is the distance
from $0$ to the segment between $1$ and $1+\hat T\rho$. The bound
\eqref{eq:aheadbound} is \eqref{eq:predict} with the record error $\tau$
added.

\begin{proof}
The controllers $C_\lambda=C(1+\lambda\rho)=(1-\lambda)C+\lambda C'$ move
from $C$ to $C'$ as $\lambda$ goes from $0$ to $1$. Each has a positive
integral gain, and its other poles are those of $C_0$ and $C_0'$, inside
the unit disc. So $L_\lambda=C_\lambda P$ has no poles outside the
indented circle of Proposition~\ref{prop:backoff}, and on the indentation it has
a large positive real part. By \eqref{eq:predict} with $\lambda\rho$ in
place of $\rho$, $1+L_\lambda=(1+L)(1+\lambda T\rho)$. Here $1+L\neq0$,
and since $|T-\hat T|=|S-\hat S|\le\tau$,
$|1+\lambda T\rho|\ge|1+\lambda\hat T\rho|-\lambda\tau|\rho|\ge d>0$. So
$1+L_\lambda\neq0$ on the indented circle for every $\lambda$, and the
number of times its Nyquist curve encircles $0$ cannot change with
$\lambda$. The loop is stable at $\lambda=0$, hence at $\lambda=1$. The
bound \eqref{eq:aheadbound} follows from \eqref{eq:predict},
$|S|\le|\hat S|+\tau$ and $|1+T\rho|\ge|1+\hat T\rho|-\tau|\rho|$.
\end{proof}

In practice, \eqref{eq:ahead} and \eqref{eq:aheadbound} are checked on
the grid of Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}, with margins for the gaps between
grid points; above $\bar\theta$, $|S-1|\le r=B/\sin(\theta/2)$ (proof
of Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}) gives $|T|\le r$, so \eqref{eq:ahead} holds
if $r|\rho|<1$, and $|S'|\le(1+r)/(1-r|\rho|)$. Since
\eqref{eq:predict} is exact, Theorem~\ref{thm:ahead} needs no small-step
assumption; a larger change only widens the bound through $\tau|\rho|$.
As $\tau\to0$, \eqref{eq:aheadbound} becomes
$\max_\theta|\hat S/(1+\hat T\rho)|$, which we call the
\emph{prediction} of $M_s(C')$. A uniform back-off is the case
$\rho=-\beta$.

\section{Application to \spin}
\label{sec:app}

\subsection{The \spin method}

\spin \cite{SPIN} tunes an I, PI or PID controller with the integral,
proportional and derivative gains $K_i$, $K_p$, $K_d$. In each
iteration, it applies one setpoint step, records the error $e_n$, and
forms three phase portraits, curves in the plane traced as $n$ grows
(the Pachner plots of \cite{SPIN}):
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma_0&:\big(e_n,\textstyle\sum_{m\le n}e_m\big),\qquad
\Gamma_1:(\Delta e_n,e_n),\\
\Gamma_2&:(\Delta^2e_n,\Delta e_n),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:portraits}
\end{equation}
with the differences $\Delta e_n=e_n-e_{n-1}=a_n$ and
$\Delta^2e_n=\Delta e_n-\Delta e_{n-1}$. $\Gamma_0$, $\Gamma_1$ and
$\Gamma_2$ emphasize low, middle and high frequencies, where the
integral, proportional and derivative actions dominate the controller;
we call them bands $0$, $1$ and $2$. Each axis is divided by its peak,
and the count $N_k$ is the number of turns that $\Gamma_k$ makes around
its end point until the error stays within $\pm\delta$ of the setpoint
($\delta$ is $2\%$ of the step). In short, \spin raises all gains while
every portrait turns little, and otherwise lowers the gain of the lowest
band that turns too much; Algorithm~1 states the rule.\footnote{An online simulator:
\url{https://robopid-simulator.uceeb.cvut.cz}}

\subsection{From certified to safe \spin}
\label{sec:spin}

Algorithm~1 adds the tests of Section~\ref{sec:accept} in two steps.
\emph{Certified \spin accepts a tuning only if it passes both the counts
and the test.} It adds layer A: the counts shape the tuning, and the test
decides whether it is accepted. A tuning that passes the counts but fails
the test is lowered by the band move that \eqref{eq:predict} predicts to
lower $M_s$, or else by a back-off (A2).

\begin{corollary}[Certified \spin]
\label{cor:spin}
Under the conditions of Proposition~\ref{prop:backoff}, the result of
certified \spin has $M_s\le M^*$, whatever the limits $\bar N_k$. If no
gain reaches the gain box, the test never traps \spin: every failed
tuning is followed by a tuning that passes after finitely many moves.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The result is an accepted tuning, so it passed the test, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:spin} applies. After at most $10$ band moves, A2 backs
off, which leads to a tuning that passes within $j^*$ back-offs by
Proposition~\ref{prop:backoff}(ii), unless line 2 or 6 moves the gains
first.
\end{proof}

Certified \spin ends safe, but it learns that a tuning is fragile only
by applying it. \emph{Safe \spin certifies every move before applying
it.} It adds layer B: it applies the test to every record and returns to
the last tuning that passed when one fails (B1; A2 then never acts), so
moves start only from tunings that passed; and it applies a move only if
Theorem~\ref{thm:ahead} certifies it (B2).

\begin{corollary}[Safe \spin]
\label{cor:safe}
Let the plant be stable with $K_iP(1)>0$, let the first tuning that
passes the test stabilize it, and let the record of every stable loop
meet Assumption~\ref{ass:tail}. Then, from that tuning on, every tuning
that safe \spin applies stabilizes the plant and has $M_s\le M^*$,
whatever the limits $\bar N_k$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By induction over the iterations. Let the tuning $C$ in use be stable;
by B1, either $C$ passes, so $M_s(C)\le M^*$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:spin}),
or the next tuning is the last one that passed, stable with
$M_s\le M^*$. If $C$ passes, B2 applies either $C$ itself or a move that
Theorem~\ref{thm:ahead}, from the record of $C$, certifies to be stable
with $M_s\le M^*$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Why the turn counts alone cannot certify}
\label{sec:count}

The counts see setpoint crossings, and crossings reveal $M_s$ only on
special loops. To show this, write a portrait
\eqref{eq:portraits} as $(p,q)$, with $q$ the running sum of $p$,
shifted so that the end point is the origin. The count is the net
counterclockwise angle swept around the origin, divided by $2\pi$. We
state the results in continuous time, where $\dot q=p$. For $\Gamma_0$,
$p=e$, and its zeros are the setpoint crossings.

\begin{lemma}[Half turns]
\label{lem:half}
Let $\dot q=p$ with $p$ continuous, let $p(t_1)=p(t_2)=0$ with
$q(t_1)q(t_2)<0$, and let $p\neq0$ on $(t_1,t_2)$. Then, on
$[t_1,t_2]$, the curve $(p,q)$ turns counterclockwise around the origin
by exactly half a turn, and so does its image under any linear map with
a positive determinant, such as the per-axis scaling of \spin.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On $(t_1,t_2)$, $p$ keeps one sign, so the curve stays in one half-plane
bounded by the $q$-axis, and $q$ moves monotonically from one ray of the
$q$-axis to the opposite ray. The curve avoids the origin, since $p=0$
only at $t_1$ and $t_2$, where $q\neq0$. The angle thus changes by
exactly $\pi$, counterclockwise, since $q$ increases where $p>0$ and
decreases where $p<0$. A linear map with a positive determinant maps
half-planes to half-planes and opposite rays to opposite rays, and keeps
the orientation.
\end{proof}

So each setpoint crossing adds half a turn to $N_0$, and a count sees
the crossings that rise above $\delta$. Whether crossings reveal $M_s$
depends on the loop. We first study a loop where they do. Lambda, IMC
and SIMC tuning rules cancel the plant lag by the controller zero
\cite{Skogestad2003}, e.g., PI with the integral time $T_i=T_1$ on the
plant $K\mathrm{e}^{-sL_d}/(T_1s+1)$. The loop is then
\begin{equation}
L(s)=\frac{\kappa\,\mathrm{e}^{-sL_d}}{sL_d},\qquad \kappa=\frac{KK_pL_d}{T_i}.
\label{eq:loop}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Crossings on lag-cancelled loops]
\label{prop:exact}
The loop \eqref{eq:loop} is stable for $0<\kappa<\pi/2$. Its $M_s$
strictly increases with $\kappa$ and is given by
\begin{equation}
\kappa=x\sin x-x^2\cos x,\qquad
\frac{1}{M_s^2}=1-\frac{2\kappa\sin x}{x}+\frac{\kappa^2}{x^2},
\label{eq:param}
\end{equation}
where $x\in(0,\pi/2)$ is the peak frequency normalized as $x=\omega L_d$.
The error after a unit setpoint step never crosses the setpoint if and
only if $\kappa\le1/\mathrm{e}$, that is, $M_s\le1.394$; otherwise it
crosses infinitely often.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At $x=\omega L_d$, $L$ has the phase $-\pi/2-x$ and the gain $\kappa/x$.
The phase first reaches $-\pi$ at $x=\pi/2$, where the gain is
$2\kappa/\pi<1$, so the loop is stable. Let
$f(x)=|1+L|^2=1-2\kappa\sin x/x+\kappa^2/x^2$, so that
$M_s^2=1/\min_xf$. Then $f'(x)=2\kappa(\varphi(x)-\kappa)/x^3$ with
$\varphi(x)=x\sin x-x^2\cos x$, which increases on $(0,\pi)$ and has
$\varphi(\pi/2)=\pi/2>\kappa$. So $f$ has one minimum on $(0,\pi)$, at
the $x^*\in(0,\pi/2)$ with $\varphi(x^*)=\kappa$. Elsewhere, $f$ is
larger: $f\ge1$ on $[\pi,2\pi]$, where $\sin x\le0$, and for $x\ge2\pi$,
$f\ge(1-\kappa/x)^2>(1-2\kappa/\pi)^2=f(\pi/2)$. This gives
\eqref{eq:param}. As $\kappa$ grows, $f(x^*)$ falls, since its
derivative is $\partial f/\partial\kappa=-2\cos x^*<0$ (envelope
theorem). For the crossings, the output obeys
$\dot y(t)=(\kappa/L_d)\,e(t-L_d)$, so the error obeys
$\dot e(t)=-(\kappa/L_d)\,e(t-L_d)$ with $e=1$ on $[0,L_d]$. It stays
positive if and only if its characteristic equation
$s+(\kappa/L_d)\mathrm{e}^{-sL_d}=0$ has a real root; with $w=sL_d$, this reads
$-w\mathrm{e}^w=\kappa$, and $-w\mathrm{e}^w$ has the maximum $1/\mathrm{e}$ over real $w$.
Otherwise all solutions oscillate \cite{GyoriLadas1991}.
\end{proof}

So, at $\delta=0.02$, the counts of the loop \eqref{eq:loop} rise in
steps as $M_s$ grows (Fig.~\ref{fig:stair}(a)): with $n$ crossings
larger than $\delta$, $N_0$ and $N_1$ lie within a quarter turn above
$n/2$, and $N_2$ is $n/2-1/4$ within $0.01$ ($\Gamma_2$ starts with a
quarter turn clockwise, as $\dot e$ jumps at $t=L_d$). The limits
$\bar N_0=1/2$, $\bar N_1=3/4$, $\bar N_2=1$ of \cite{SPIN} allow $0$,
$1$ and $2$ crossings, that is, $M_s\le1.534$, $1.842$ and $2.210$;
$\Gamma_0$ binds first, so on these loops \spin stops at
$M_s\le1.534$.
% check_result1.py (Lemma 2 within 1e-6, (9) against a direct search), confirm_limits.py

On general loops, this guarantee is lost. The counts leave the steps
(in the $3800$ count decisions of certified \spin in
Section~\ref{sec:sim}, $N_0$ and $N_2$ lay off them in $23$ and $69\%$),
and crossings do not bound $M_s$: the basic tuning of
Fig.~\ref{fig:record} never crosses the setpoint, yet has $M_s=1.81$
(a PI loop on a first-order plant reaches $M_s=2.62$ without a crossing
above $2\%$), while a controller zero can cause crossings at a low
$M_s$. The counts can shape
a tuning, but only a certificate can certify it.

\section{Simulations}
\label{sec:sim}

\paragraph*{Setup} We test the three variants on the $19$ loops of
Table~\ref{tab:loops}. Each runs $200$ iterations of Algorithm~1 with its
defaults. The gain box ties the result to the start, so every loop runs
from two starts: SIMC \cite{Skogestad2003} on a half-rule
first-order-plus-dead-time model, with the closed-loop time constant
three times the model dead time (cautious), and the same tuning with all
gains doubled (bold). P, I and D act on the error. As in a real
controller, the sampling period stays fixed, and each record covers $15$
times the dead time plus the lag time constants. $M_s$ is that of this
digital loop, computed from its exact $L$. Performance is measured by
the load-step IAE, the integral of $|y|$ after a unit step disturbance
at the plant input.

\paragraph*{The certificates held and were tight} For all $276$ accepted
tunings, a four times longer record showed a tail below $3\cdot10^{-5}$,
well within $\tau=10^{-3}$, and every $M_s$ lay between its bounds, at
most $0.003$ below $\overline M$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}). Of $456$
random moves from accepted tunings, with gains scaled by up to $8$, all
$339$ certified moves were stable and all $117$ rejected ones unstable,
and the bound \eqref{eq:aheadbound} exceeded $M_s$ by a median of $0.011$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:ahead}).
% confirm_thm1.py (RUNS=safe1,safe2), confirm_thm3.py

\paragraph*{Certified \spin ends below $M^*$, only safe \spin stays below it}
Table~\ref{tab:spin} shows both corollaries. Basic \spin ended above
$M^*$ on $9$ and $7$ of the $19$ loops; certified \spin on none, and
every loop kept accepting tunings (Corollary~\ref{cor:spin}). But
certified \spin must apply a tuning to reject it, so it still applied
tunings above $M^*$ in $45\%$ of its iterations, against $57\%$ for basic
\spin. Safe \spin applied none, and all its tunings were stable
(Corollary~\ref{cor:safe}; every start tuning passed the test, so the
corollary covered every iteration).
% run_safe.sh, analyze_safe.py

\paragraph*{Robustness costs little with safe \spin} Figs.~\ref{fig:certified}--\ref{fig:response}
follow $P_3$ with PID. From the record of the basic tuning, \eqref{eq:predict} predicted
the $M_s$ after a band move exactly (Fig.~\ref{fig:certified}).
Basic and certified \spin climb alike (Fig.~\ref{fig:history});
certified \spin then lowers $K_i$, but it must apply each tuning of its
final cycle to reject it, so $131$ of its $200$ applied tunings exceed
$M^*$. Safe \spin applies only certified moves and trades $K_p$ for
$K_d$. The setpoint responses are alike (Fig.~\ref{fig:response}), and
the basic tuning does not overshoot although its $M_s$ is $1.81$: the
response alone does not reveal fragility. In load rejection, certified
\spin, with its lower $K_i$, is $23\%$ worse than basic \spin, and safe
\spin only $3\%$. Over all loops, safe and certified \spin have the same
median load-step IAE; the mean of safe \spin is higher only because of
the fast PID loop, where it settled at $M_s\approx1.5$.

\paragraph*{Count limits do not replace the certificate} With every
$\bar N_k=1/2$, certified \spin still applied tunings above $M^*$ in
$1310$ and $1420$ iterations, accepted no tuning on $2$ and $3$ loops,
and raised the median load-step IAE by $11$ and $19\%$.
% run_limits_fixed.sh, analyze_limits_fixed.py

\section{Conclusion}

The setpoint step that a model-free tuner applies anyway certifies the
robustness of the loop: its record bounds $M_s$ from both sides, without
a model or a dedicated experiment, and certifies the stability and $M_s$
of the next tuning before it is applied. Checking a tuning after
applying it makes the result safe; checking it before makes the whole
tuning safe. The theorems assume a
linear loop and a long enough record; noise and real loops are left to
a follow-up.
