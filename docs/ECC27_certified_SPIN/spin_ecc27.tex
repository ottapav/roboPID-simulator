\begin{abstract}
The \spin method tunes a PID controller without a model. After each
setpoint step it counts the turns of the control error in three phase
portraits and raises the gains until a count exceeds its limit. The
limits were calibrated, and nothing guaranteed robustness. We prove two
results. First, for any loop, the count of the integral portrait is
within half a turn of half the number of setpoint crossings whenever the
lobes of the error shrink. On loops that
cancel the plant lags, which have a single gain parameter, the lobes always
shrink in the relevant range, the sensitivity peak $M_s$ grows strictly
with the gain, and the error never crosses the setpoint if and only if
$M_s\le1.394$. Second, for any stable digital loop with one degree of freedom, the increments of the
error after a unit setpoint step are the impulse response of the
sensitivity function. From one finite record we derive a lower and an
upper bound on $M_s$ that need no model and one assumption on the
variation of the error after the record ends, and both converge to $M_s$.
Used as the acceptance test of \spin, the upper bound certifies every
accepted tuning, the same record predicts the effect of lowering any
gain, and every run of rejections by the test ends: the test never
traps the original rules. Simulations on $19$ loops confirm the theory.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Let $L=CP$ be the loop transfer function and $S=1/(1+L)$ the sensitivity
function. The peak $M_s=\max_\omega|S|$ measures robustness: a value
near $1.4$ is safe, a value near $2$ is close to the limit
\cite{AstromHagglund2004}. Tuning rules reach a chosen $M_s$ with a model
\cite{AstromHagglund2004,Skogestad2003}. After tuning, plants rarely
check $M_s$, because a check needs a model or a test that disturbs
production \cite{Desborough2002,Bauer2016}.

\subsection{The \spin method}

\spin \cite{SPIN} tunes a PI or PID controller with gains $K_i,K_p,K_d$
without a model. In each iteration it applies one setpoint step, records
the control error $e$, and forms three phase portraits,
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_0:(e,\textstyle\sum e),\quad \Gamma_1:(\Delta e,e),\quad
\Gamma_2:(\Delta^2e,\Delta e),
\label{eq:portraits}
\end{equation}
where $\sum e$ is the running sum of $e$ and $\Delta$ the difference
between samples. The portraits $\Gamma_0,\Gamma_1,\Gamma_2$ react to the
bands of $K_i$, $K_p$, $K_d$, called bands $0,1,2$.
The turn count $N_k$ is the number of turns that $\Gamma_k$ makes around
its end point before the error stays within a band $\delta$ ($2\%$ of the
step by default). Algorithm~1 gives the rule. \spin needs no model and no
plant-specific setting \cite{SPIN}, but its limits $\bar N_k$ were chosen
by calibration, and nothing guaranteed the robustness of the result. An
online simulator lets the reader try
\spin.\footnote{\url{https://robopid-simulator.uceeb.cvut.cz}}

\subsection{Contributions}

\emph{Result~1: what a count means} (Section~\ref{sec:count}).
Lemma~\ref{lem:half} shows, for any loop, that a portrait turns by exactly
half a turn between two zeros of its first coordinate, in any linear
coordinates; so the count of $\Gamma_0$ is within half a turn of half the
number of setpoint crossings when the lobes of the error shrink
(Corollary~\ref{cor:count}). For loops that
cancel the plant lags we prove that the lobes shrink, that $M_s$ grows
strictly with the loop gain, and that the error crosses the setpoint if and
only if $M_s>1.394$ (Propositions~\ref{prop:exact}, \ref{prop:cross}).
The Lambert-$W$ and $1/e$ facts are classical
\cite{AslUlsoy2003,GyoriLadas1991}; their link to the counts is new.

\emph{Result~2: the step certifies $M_s$} (Sections~\ref{sec:record},
\ref{sec:spin}). For a digital loop the record gives the sensitivity
exactly (Lemma~\ref{lem:record}). Theorem~\ref{thm:cert} turns one finite
record into certified lower and upper bounds on $M_s$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:spin} makes them the acceptance test of \spin: every
accepted tuning satisfies $M_s\le M^*$, every run of rejections by the
test ends, and no sequence of rejections can return to the same gains.
Proposition~\ref{prop:shape} predicts from the same record the effect of
lowering any gain. Section~\ref{sec:sim}
confirms all statements in simulation.

\subsection{Related work}

Frequency responses have been computed from step and relay responses
\cite{WangHangBi1997}, sensitivity functions and margins from closed-loop
data with reference excitation or designed experiments
\cite{VandenHofSchrama1993,Isoshima2023}. The
setpoint overshoot method tunes PI controllers from one setpoint step
\cite{Shamsuzzoha2010}. Model-free tuning by repeated experiments
\cite{IFT1998,Killingsworth2006} or from one data set \cite{VRFT2002}
optimizes performance. Here one normal setpoint step, without special
excitation, gives a certified bound on $M_s$ inside a model-free rule.

\section{Result 1: What a Turn Count Means}
\label{sec:count}

\subsection{Half turns}

In each portrait \eqref{eq:portraits}, the second coordinate $q$ is the
running sum of the first, $p$; in continuous time $\dot q=p$, and the turns
are counted around the end point, which we place at the origin. We state
the results in continuous time; the sampled counts of \spin approximate
them. For $\Gamma_0$ the zeros of $p=e$ are the setpoint crossings, for
$\Gamma_1$ the extrema of $e$.

\begin{lemma}[Half turns]
\label{lem:half}
Let $\dot q=p$ with $p$ continuous, and let $t_1<t_2$ be consecutive zeros
of $p$ with $q(t_1)q(t_2)<0$. On $[t_1,t_2]$ the curve $(p,q)$ turns
counterclockwise around the origin by exactly half a turn. So does its
image under any linear map with positive determinant, such as the
whitening of \cite{SPIN}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On $(t_1,t_2)$, $p$ keeps one sign, so the curve stays in one open
half-plane bounded by the $q$-axis, and $q$ moves monotonically from
$q(t_1)$ to $q(t_2)$, that is from one ray of the $q$-axis to the opposite
ray. In the half-plane the angle lies in an open interval of length $\pi$,
so it changes by exactly $\pi$; for $p>0$, $q$ increases, and for $p<0$ it
decreases, so the turn is counterclockwise in both cases. A linear map
with positive determinant maps the half-plane to a half-plane and the two
rays to opposite rays, and it keeps the orientation.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Counts and crossings]
\label{cor:count}
Let $p$ change sign at $t_1<\dots<t_m$, $m\ge1$, before the record
settles, let
$q(t_j)$ alternate in sign, and let $q(t_1)$ have the sign of $p$ before
$t_1$. Then the turn count of $(p,q)$ lies in
$(\frac{m-1}{2},\frac{m+1}{2})$. For $\Gamma_0$, these conditions hold
when the areas of the lobes of $e$ between crossings decrease strictly.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Each of the $m-1$ intervals adds $\pi$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:half}). Before
$t_1$ and after $t_m$ the curve stays in one open half-plane and starts
or ends on the ray from which it turns counterclockwise, so each end adds
an angle in $(0,\pi)$. For $\Gamma_0$, $q(t_j)=-\int_{t_j}^\infty e\,dt$
is an alternating sum of lobe areas; when they decrease, it has the sign
of its first term.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The lag-cancelled loop}

Lambda, IMC and SIMC tunings cancel the plant lags \cite{Skogestad2003}:
PI $K_c(1+1/(T_is))$ with $T_i=T$ on $Ke^{-sL_d}/(Ts+1)$, or series PID
with $T_i=T_1$, $T_d=T_2$ on a plant with two lags. The loop is then
\begin{equation}
L(s)=\frac{k\,e^{-sL_d}}{sL_d},\qquad k=\frac{KK_cL_d}{T_i}.
\label{eq:loop}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Exact robustness]
\label{prop:exact}
The loop \eqref{eq:loop} is stable for $0<k<\pi/2$, with gain margin
$\pi/(2k)$ and poles $W_j(-k)/L_d$, where $W_j$ are the branches of the
Lambert $W$ function \cite{AslUlsoy2003}. Its peak $M_s$ is a strictly
increasing function of $k$, given by
\begin{equation}
k=x\sin x-x^2\cos x,\qquad
\frac{1}{M_s^2}=1-\frac{2k\sin x}{x}+\frac{k^2}{x^2},
\label{eq:param}
\end{equation}
where $x=\omega_pL_d\in(0,\pi/2)$ is the normalized peak frequency.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The phase of $L$ is $-\pi/2-x$; it reaches $-\pi$ at $x=\pi/2$, where
$|L|=2k/\pi$. The poles solve $sL_de^{sL_d}=-k$. Let
$h(x)=|1+L|^2=1-2k\sin x/x+k^2/x^2$. Then
$h'(x)=2k(\varphi(x)-k)/x^3$ with $\varphi(x)=x\sin x-x^2\cos x$, which
increases on $(0,\pi)$ with $\varphi(\pi/2)=\pi/2>k$. So $h$ has a single
minimum $x^*\in(0,\pi/2)$ on $(0,\pi)$. On $[\pi,2\pi]$, $h\ge1$; for
$x\ge2\pi$, $h\ge(1-k/x)^2>(1-2k/\pi)^2=h(\pi/2)$. So $x^*$ is the global
minimum, which gives \eqref{eq:param}. By the envelope theorem,
$\frac{d}{dk}h(x^*)=\frac{2}{x^{*2}}(k-x^*\sin x^*)=-2\cos x^*<0$.
\end{proof}

SIMC with $\tau_c=L_d$ and $T\le8L_d$ cancels the lag and gives
$k=\frac12$, so $\mathrm{GM}=\pi$ and $M_s=1.59$ by \eqref{eq:param},
exactly the values in \cite[Table~2]{SkogestadGrimholt2012}.

After a unit setpoint step, $e=1$ on $[0,L_d]$ and
$\dot e(t)=-(k/L_d)\,e(t-L_d)$. Let $c_1<c_2<\dots$ be the setpoint
crossings, $c_0=0$, lobe $i$ the interval $[c_i,c_{i+1}]$, and $E_0=1$.

\begin{proposition}[Crossings]
\label{prop:cross}
For the loop \eqref{eq:loop} with $0<k<\pi/2$:
(a) the error never crosses the setpoint if and only if $k\le1/e$, that is
$M_s\le M_s(1/e)=1.394$; for $k>1/e$ it crosses infinitely often.
(b) Lobe $i$ contains exactly one extremum $E_i$, at $c_i+L_d$.
(c) $|E_{i+1}|\le r(k)\,|E_i|$, with $r(k)=k^2/2$ for $k\le1$ and
$r(k)=k-\frac12$ for $k\ge1$.
(d) For $k<3/2$ the lobe areas decrease strictly, so
Corollary~\ref{cor:count} holds for $\Gamma_0$: $N_0$ is within half a
turn of half the number of crossings.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(a) The record is the fundamental solution of the delay equation. It is
positive if and only if $s+(k/L_d)e^{-sL_d}=0$ has a real root, that is
$k\le1/e$, and for $k>1/e$ all solutions oscillate \cite{GyoriLadas1991};
$M_s$ follows from Proposition~\ref{prop:exact}. (b) $\dot e(t)$ has the
sign of $-e(t-L_d)$. By induction over the lobes, $e$ moves away from zero
on $(c_i,c_i+L_d)$, where $e(t-L_d)$ lies in lobe $i-1$, so lobe $i$ is
longer than $L_d$; and $e$ moves toward zero on $(c_i+L_d,c_{i+1}+L_d)$,
where $e(t-L_d)$ lies in lobe $i$. For $i=0$ the extremum is the plateau
$e=1$ on $[0,L_d]$. (c) On lobe $i$, $|e|\le|E_i|$. After the
extremum, $|\dot e|\le(k/L_d)|E_i|$, so $e$ needs at least $L_d/k$ to reach
zero and $|e(t)|\le|E_i|\min\{1,k(c_{i+1}-t)/L_d\}$ on the last $L_d$ of
the lobe. Since $e(c_{i+1})=0$,
$E_{i+1}=-\frac{k}{L_d}\int_{c_{i+1}-L_d}^{c_{i+1}}e\,dt$, and the bound
integrates to $r(k)|E_i|$. (d) Integrating $\dot e$ from $c_i+L_d$ to
$c_{i+1}+L_d$ gives the area $\frac{L_d}{k}(|E_i|+|E_{i+1}|)$ of lobe $i$,
as the extrema alternate in sign. By (c), $|E_{i+2}|<|E_i|$
for $r(k)<1$, that is $k<3/2$, where $M_s=26.2$.
\end{proof}

So on lag-cancelled loops $N_0$ reads the number of crossings, and
crossings read $M_s$: none if and only if $M_s\le1.394$, and by (c) the
extrema decay at least geometrically. Fig.~\ref{fig:stair} illustrates
both statements.

\subsection{Counts do not certify in general}

Outside lag-cancelled loops, crossings and $M_s$ are not tied. A
first-order plant with $T=0.3L_d$ under PI with $T_i=8T$ and gain margin
$1.624$ has $M_s=2.62$, yet its error never crosses the setpoint by more
than $2\%$; the oscillation rides on a slow decay ($M_s$ of the
continuous loop; $2.61$ for the digital loop of Fig.~\ref{fig:record}).
Conversely, overshoot from a controller zero can produce crossings at a
low $M_s$. So counts can guide
tuning but cannot certify it. The same step, however,
contains $M_s$.

\section{Result 2: The Same Step Certifies $M_s$}
\label{sec:record}

A digital controller runs at sampling period $T_s$. The loop it closes,
seen at the samples, is linear and time invariant. From here on $L(z)$,
$P(z)$, $S=1/(1+L)$ and $M_s=\max_{\theta\in[0,\pi]}|S(e^{j\theta})|$,
$\theta=\omega T_s$, denote this loop, the loop that actually runs.

\begin{lemma}[Exact identity]
\label{lem:record}
Let the loop be stable, with one degree of freedom and strictly proper
$L$ (the measurement precedes the control action), and let $e_n$ be the
error after a unit setpoint step at $n=0$ from rest, without disturbances,
so $e_{-1}=0$ and $e_0=1$; let $a_n=\Delta e_n=e_n-e_{n-1}$. Then
\begin{equation}
S(e^{j\theta})=\sum_{n\ge0}a_n\,e^{-jn\theta}.
\label{eq:srecord}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$E(z)=S(z)R(z)$ with $R(z)=1/(1-z^{-1})$, so $S=(1-z^{-1})E$. In a
stable loop $a_n$ decays exponentially, so the series converges on the
unit circle.
\end{proof}

In continuous time the same argument gives
$S(j\omega)=\int_0^\infty e^{-j\omega t}de(t)$. A record is finite. The only quantity that it cannot
give is the variation of the error after it ends.

\begin{assumption}[Settled record]
\label{ass:tail}
The loop is stable, and the record $e_0,\dots,e_N$ satisfies
$\sum_{n>N}|a_n|\le\tau$ for a known $\tau$.
\end{assumption}

In a stable loop $|a_n|\le c\rho^n$ with $\rho<1$, so the tail variation
falls geometrically with the record length; $\tau$ states how settled
the record is, in units of the step.

\begin{theorem}[Certified bounds from one record]
\label{thm:cert}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:tail}, let $\hat S(\theta)=\sum_{n=0}^{N}
a_ne^{-jn\theta}$, let $G$ be a uniform grid on $[0,\bar\theta]$, $\bar\theta\le\pi$, with spacing $h$ and both ends, and let
\begin{align*}
J&=\textstyle\sum_{n=0}^N|n-n_c|\,|a_n|,\;\;
B=\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}|a_{n+1}-a_n|+|a_N|+2\tau,
\end{align*}
for any $n_c$. Then $\underline M\le M_s\le\overline M$, with
\begin{align}
\underline M&=\textstyle\max_{\theta\in G}|\hat S(\theta)|-\tau,\nonumber\\
\overline M&=\max\Big\{\textstyle\max_{\theta\in G}|\hat S(\theta)|+\frac{hJ}{2}+\tau,\;
1+\frac{B}{\sin(\bar\theta/2)}\Big\},
\label{eq:bound}
\end{align}
where the second term is dropped if $\bar\theta=\pi$. With $\tau$ equal to
the true tail, $N\to\infty$, $h\to0$ and $\bar\theta=\pi$, both bounds
converge to $M_s$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:srecord}, $|S-\hat S|\le\sum_{n>N}|a_n|\le\tau$ at every
$\theta$, which gives $\underline M$. On $[0,\bar\theta]$, $|\hat S|$
equals $|e^{jn_c\theta}\hat S|$, whose derivative is bounded by $J$; every
$\theta$ is within $h/2$ of a grid point, which gives the first term of
$\overline M$. On $[\bar\theta,\pi]$, let $z=e^{-j\theta}$ and
$Z_n=\sum_{m=1}^nz^m$, so $|Z_n|\le2/|1-z|=1/\sin(\theta/2)$. Summation
by parts gives $\sum_{n\ge1}a_nz^n=\sum_{n\ge1}(a_n-a_{n+1})Z_n$, and
$\sum_{n\ge N}|a_n-a_{n+1}|\le|a_N|+2\tau$, so
$|S-a_0|\le B/\sin(\theta/2)$ with $a_0=1$. Convergence: the true
tail and $hJ$ tend to zero.
\end{proof}

Every quantity in \eqref{eq:bound} except $\tau$ comes from the record;
the grid values are one chirp $z$-transform. The second term needs no
grid: for a smooth record $B$ is small and certifies all high frequencies
at once.

\section{Certified \spin}
\label{sec:spin}

\begin{figure}[t]
\small
\hrule\smallskip

becomes
\smallskip\hrule\smallskip
\noindent\begin{tabular}{@{}r@{\hspace{0.6em}}l@{}}
7a: & compute $\overline M$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}: $\bar\theta$ solves\\
    & $1+B/\sin(\bar\theta/2)=M^*$ ($\bar\theta=\pi$ if none), $hJ/2\le\eta$\\
7b: & \textbf{if} $\overline M\le M^*$ \textbf{then} raise all
gains by one step \hfill (accept)\\
7c: & \textbf{else} predict, by Proposition~\ref{prop:shape},
$M_s$ after\\
    & \spin's move of each band (lower it, raise those below)\\
7d: & apply the highest band predicted to lower $M_s$, at most\\
    & $n_{\max}$ times between acceptances; otherwise lower all\\
    & gains by one step; end the iteration\\
\end{tabular}
\smallskip\hrule\smallskip
\noindent Defaults: $M^*=1.7$, $\tau=10^{-3}$, $\eta=2\cdot10^{-3}$,
$n_{\max}=10$.
\smallskip\hrule
\end{figure}

Algorithm~2 changes only line 7 of \spin. The counts still shape the
tuning; the bound decides whether it is accepted. On a rejection it uses
\spin's own two moves: lowering one band while raising those below it,
or lowering all gains.

\begin{theorem}[Certified \spin]
\label{thm:spin}
Let the plant be stable, let $K_iP(1)>0$, let
Assumption~\ref{ass:tail} hold for every record, let $hJ/2\le\eta$, and
for (iii), (iv) let no gain be clipped by the box of Algorithm~1.
(i) Every tuning accepted by Algorithm~2 has $M_s\le M^*$.
(ii) For gains $K$, let $m=\min\{0,\inf_{0<\theta\le\pi}\mathrm{Re}\,L(e^{j\theta})\}$.
For $0<\lambda<1/|m|$ the loop with gains $\lambda K$ is stable and
\begin{equation}
M_s(\lambda K)\le\frac{1}{1-\lambda|m|}.
\label{eq:backoff}
\end{equation}
(iii) If $M^*>1+2\tau+\eta$, every run of consecutive rejections by the
test of line 7b ends, in an acceptance or in a rejection by the counts
(lines 2--10 of Algorithm~1). It has at most $n_{\max}$ band moves, and
each stretch of uniform back-offs has at most
$\lceil\ln\frac{|m|M'}{M'-1}/\ln\frac1{1-\beta}\rceil$ of them,
$M'=M^*-2\tau-\eta$, with $m$ of the gains where the stretch starts.
(iv) Every move except an acceptance lowers
$\Phi=\ln K_i+2\ln K_p+4\ln K_d$ by at least $\ln\frac1{1-\beta}$, so the
gains never return to earlier values without an acceptance in between.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}. (ii) $L$ is bounded on the unit circle
except near $z=1$, where the integral term has a bounded real part
because $P$ is analytic there and
$\mathrm{Re}\,1/(e^{j\theta}-1)=-\frac12$; so $m$ is finite. Then
$|1+\lambda L|\ge1+\lambda\,\mathrm{Re}\,L\ge1-\lambda|m|>0$, which
gives \eqref{eq:backoff}. The Nyquist curve of $\lambda L$ lies in
$\mathrm{Re}\ge-\lambda|m|>-1$, also on the indentation at $z=1$, where
$K_iP(1)>0$ makes the real part large and positive; it cannot encircle
$-1$, and $L$ has no poles outside the unit disc, so the loop is
stable. (iii) Line 7d allows at most $n_{\max}$ band moves. In a stretch of
uniform back-offs from gains $K$, after $j$ of them $\lambda=(1-\beta)^j$,
and \eqref{eq:backoff} gives $M_s\le M'$ for the stated $j$. In
\eqref{eq:bound} the grid term is then at most $M_s+\tau+\eta+\tau\le
M^*$, and the second term is at most $M^*$ by the choice of $\bar\theta$, so
the test passes unless the counts reject first. (iv) A band move changes
$\Phi$ by $\ln\frac1{1-\beta}(\sum_{j<k}w_j-w_k)$ with $w=(1,2,4)$, which
is at most $-\ln\frac1{1-\beta}$; lowering all gains changes it by
$-7\ln\frac1{1-\beta}$, and line 3 by $-17\ln2$. A repeated state
needs $\sum\Delta\Phi=0$, hence an acceptance, which raises $\Phi$.
\end{proof}

So the test never traps \spin: it either certifies a tuning or returns
control to the original rules. Part (i) holds with or without the box. Part (iv)
does not exclude a drift without acceptance, in which $\Phi$ falls
forever; that is a property of the counts and is stopped by the gain box
of \spin, as Section~\ref{sec:sim} shows.

A uniform back-off is safe by (ii) but costs load rejection; lowering one
band is often cheaper, and the same record tells what each move does.

\begin{proposition}[Prediction from one step]
\label{prop:shape}
For any controller $C'$ on the same plant,
$S'=S/(S+(1-S)C'/C)$ exactly. If the record gives
$\hat S=S+\varepsilon$, the prediction $\hat S'$ errs by
$\hat S'-S'=\varepsilon\,(C'/C)\,S'\hat S'/(S\hat S)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$L'=LC'/C$ with $L=(1-S)/S$; subtracting the two expressions
gives the error.
\end{proof}

The prediction only selects the move; each move is checked by the next
step, so safety never depends on it.

\section{Confirmation in Simulation}
\label{sec:sim}

\paragraph*{Setup} We compare \spin as published with certified \spin
(Algorithm~2), with $200$ iterations, derivative filter ratio $10$ and
the gain box $[0.001,10]$ of \cite{SPIN}. Because the box ties the result
to the start, every loop runs from two starts: SIMC on a half-rule model
with $\tau_c$ three times its dead time (cautious), and twice as bold. The plants are
those of \cite{SPIN},
\begin{align*}
P_1&=\frac{e^{-s}}{(10s+1)(s+1)^3}, &
P_2&=\frac{1.25\,e^{-8s}}{(5s+1)^4},\\
P_3&=\frac{e^{-10s}}{(2s+1)(s+1)^3}, &
P_4&=\frac{e^{-4s}}{(8s+1)^6},
\end{align*}
with PI and PID; a delay-dominant $e^{-5s}/(s+1)$, two lag-dominant
$e^{-s}/(10s+1)$ and $e^{-0.5s}/((10s+1)(s+1))$ with PI; a fast
$e^{-0.1s}/((s+1)(0.5s+1))$ with PID; and
$e^{-L_ds}/((s+1)(0.5s+1)(0.25s+1))$ with PID and
$L_d\in\{0.25,\dots,16\}$ (dead-time share $0.125$ to $0.90$). The
controller runs at $T_s=\min(T_a/100,T_f/20)$ for PID, with $T_f$ the
derivative filter time constant and $T_a$ the plant's dead time plus its
lag time constants, and at
$T_a/1000$ for PI; each lag is discretized exactly for a piecewise-constant
input and the delay is rounded to whole samples. The record covers
$15\,T_a$.
P and D act on the error. All $M_s$ below are those of this digital
loop, computed from its exact $L$. At the end, \spin cycles between a few tunings; we report the most
aggressive one accepted in the last $60$ iterations.

\paragraph*{Result 1} On the lag-cancelled loop with $1/e<k<3/2$, the
bound (c) of Proposition~\ref{prop:cross} held for every extremum (with
equality for the first when $k\ge1$), the lobe areas decreased, each
interval between crossings turned by $\frac12$ turn to $10^{-6}$, also
after a linear map, and the whitened count of \spin satisfied
Corollary~\ref{cor:count} for all $126$ values of $k$ tested;
\eqref{eq:param} matched a direct search of $M_s$ to six digits. % check_result1.py

\paragraph*{Lemma~\ref{lem:record} and Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}} For each of
the $146$ tunings accepted in all runs of this section (both rules; cautious, bold and boxless; distinct within each run), we simulated a record four

\section{Conclusion}

The count of $\Gamma_0$ is within half a turn of half the number of
setpoint crossings when the lobes of the error shrink; on lag-cancelled
loops the crossings read $M_s$ exactly, so by eye an error that never
crosses the setpoint means $M_s\le1.394$. On any stable digital loop the
setpoint step that \spin already records gives the sensitivity function
exactly, and one finite record gives certified bounds on $M_s$; every
setpoint change in a historian, made from rest, certifies $M_s$ without a
model. As the acceptance test of \spin, the upper bound certifies every
accepted tuning, and every run of rejections ends, so the test never traps
the original rules. The theorems assume a linear loop and a settled
record; noise and real loops are the subject of a follow-up. Scripts for
every number and figure are in
\url{https://github.com/ottapav/robopid-simulator}, folder
\texttt{docs/ECC27\_certified\_SPIN}.
