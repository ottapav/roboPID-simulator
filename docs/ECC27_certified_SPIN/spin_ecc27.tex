\begin{abstract}
The \spin method tunes a PID controller without a model. After each
setpoint step, it counts how many turns the control error makes in three
phase portraits, and it raises the gains while the counts stay below
calibrated limits, so its robustness was not guaranteed. We explain why
and close the gap in three steps. First, a count
measures setpoint crossings, and crossings reveal the sensitivity peak
$M_s$ only on special loops: on loops that cancel the plant lags, the
error never crosses the setpoint if and only if $M_s\le1.394$; in general
they are not tied, so the counts cannot certify. Second, the step that \spin
already records contains $M_s$: its increments are exactly the impulse
response of the sensitivity function of the digital loop, so one finite
record certifies bounds on $M_s$ without a model. Third, certified \spin
accepts a tuning only if this upper bound is at most a target $M^*$; so
the certificate, not the counts, guarantees $M_s\le M^*$, and the test
never traps \spin. In simulations
on $19$ loops, published \spin exceeded $M^*=1.7$ on up to $9$ loops and
certified \spin on none.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Let $L=CP$ be the loop transfer function and $S=1/(1+L)$ the sensitivity
function. Its peak $M_s=\max_\omega|S|$ measures robustness: a value near
$1.4$ is safe, and a value near $2$ indicates poor robustness
\cite{AstromHagglund2004}. Tuning rules reach a chosen $M_s$ by using a
model \cite{AstromHagglund2004,Skogestad2003}. Once a loop is tuned,
$M_s$ is rarely checked, because a check needs either a model or a test
that disturbs production \cite{Desborough2002,Bauer2016}.

\subsection{The \spin method}

\spin \cite{SPIN} tunes a PI or PID controller with the gains
$K_i,K_p,K_d$ without a model. In each iteration, it applies one setpoint
step, records the control error $e$, and forms three phase portraits,
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_0:(e,\textstyle\sum e),\quad \Gamma_1:(\Delta e,e),\quad
\Gamma_2:(\Delta^2e,\Delta e),
\label{eq:portraits}
\end{equation}
where $\sum e$ is the running sum of $e$ and $\Delta$ is the difference
between consecutive samples. The portraits $\Gamma_0,\Gamma_1,\Gamma_2$
correspond to the frequency bands of $K_i$, $K_p$ and $K_d$, which we call
bands $0$, $1$ and $2$. The turn count $N_k$ is the number of turns that
$\Gamma_k$ makes around its end point before the error stays within
$\pm\delta$ ($2\%$ of the step by default). Algorithm~1 states the
rule. Its limits $\bar N_k$ were found by calibration, and
the robustness of the result was not guaranteed. An online simulator lets
the reader try \spin.\footnote{\url{https://robopid-simulator.uceeb.cvut.cz}}

\subsection{Idea and contributions}

The idea of this paper fits in one sentence: \emph{the setpoint step
that \spin records in every iteration already contains $M_s$, so \spin
can keep its counts to shape the tuning and let the record decide whether
the tuning is accepted.} Three steps, each ending in a theorem, lead
there.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Counts cannot certify} (Section~\ref{sec:count}). A count
measures setpoint crossings (Lemma~\ref{lem:half},
Corollary~\ref{cor:count}); at small $\delta$, it measures the damping of
the dominant poles (Proposition~\ref{prop:delta}). Crossings reveal $M_s$
on loops that cancel the plant lags (Theorem~\ref{thm:counts}), but not
in general.
\item \emph{One step reveals $M_s$} (Section~\ref{sec:record}). In a
digital loop, the record gives the sensitivity function exactly
(Lemma~\ref{lem:record}), and a finite record gives certified bounds on
$M_s$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}).
\item \emph{Certified \spin} (Section~\ref{sec:spin}) is \spin with one
more test: a tuning is accepted only if the upper bound from its record
is at most a target $M^*$. It accepts only tunings with $M_s\le M^*$,
and the test never traps it (Theorem~\ref{thm:spin}).
\end{enumerate}
Section~\ref{sec:sim} confirms each step in simulation.

\paragraph*{Related work} Margins have been estimated from step, relay
and closed-loop data \cite{WangHangBi1997,VandenHofSchrama1993,Isoshima2023},
PI tuned from one step \cite{Shamsuzzoha2010}, and performance optimized
without a model \cite{IFT1998,Killingsworth2006,VRFT2002}.

\section{Counts Cannot Certify}
\label{sec:count}

\subsection{Every crossing adds half a turn}

In each portrait \eqref{eq:portraits}, the second coordinate $q$ is the
running sum of the first coordinate $p$; in continuous time, $\dot q=p$.
The turn count is the net counterclockwise angle swept around the end
point, which we place at the origin, divided by $2\pi$. We state the
results in continuous time; the sampled counts of \spin approximate
them. For $\Gamma_0$, the zeros of $p=e$ are the
setpoint crossings; for $\Gamma_1$, they are the extrema of $e$.

\begin{lemma}[Half turns]
\label{lem:half}
Let $\dot q=p$ with $p$ continuous, let $p(t_1)=p(t_2)=0$ with
$q(t_1)q(t_2)<0$, and let $p$ not change sign on $(t_1,t_2)$. Then, on $[t_1,t_2]$, the curve $(p,q)$
turns counterclockwise around the origin by exactly half a turn. The same
holds for its image under any linear map with a positive determinant,
such as the whitening used in \cite{SPIN}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On $(t_1,t_2)$, $p$ keeps one sign. Hence the curve stays in one closed
half-plane bounded by the $q$-axis, and $q$ moves monotonically from
$q(t_1)$ to $q(t_2)$, that is, from one ray of the $q$-axis to the
opposite ray; the curve avoids the origin, since $q(t)=0$ only where
$p\neq0$. The angle therefore stays in a closed interval of length $\pi$
whose ends are the two rays, so it changes by exactly $\pi$. If $p>0$, $q$
increases; if $p<0$, $q$ decreases; in both cases the turn is
counterclockwise. A linear map with a positive determinant maps the
half-plane to a half-plane and the two rays to opposite rays, and it
preserves the orientation.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Counts and crossings]
\label{cor:count}
Let $p(0)\neq0$, and let $p$ change sign at $t_1<\dots<t_m$, $m\ge0$,
before the error stays within $\pm\delta$. Let $q(t_j)$ alternate in
sign, and let $q(t_1)$ have the sign of $p$ before $t_1$. Then the turn
count of $(p,q)$, and of its image under any linear map with a positive
determinant, lies in $(\frac{m-1}{2},\frac{m+1}{2})$. For $\Gamma_0$, these conditions hold if
the areas of the lobes of $e$ between crossings decrease strictly.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For $m=0$, the curve stays in one open half-plane, so its angle changes
by less than $\pi$. For $m\ge1$, by Lemma~\ref{lem:half}, each of the
$m-1$ intervals between sign changes adds $\pi$. Before $t_1$ and after
$t_m$, the curve stays in one open half-plane; by the sign conditions, it
ends (before $t_1$) or starts (after $t_m$) on the ray from which it
turns counterclockwise, so each end adds an angle in $(0,\pi)$. For $\Gamma_0$,
$q(t_j)=-\int_{t_j}^\infty e\,dt$ is an alternating sum of lobe areas; if
the areas decrease, the sum has the sign of its first term.
\end{proof}

\subsection{When crossings reveal $M_s$}

Lambda, IMC and SIMC tunings cancel the plant lags \cite{Skogestad2003},
e.g., a PI controller $K_c(1+1/(T_is))$ with $T_i=T$ on the plant
$Ke^{-sL_d}/(Ts+1)$, or a series PID controller with $T_i=T_1$ and
$T_d=T_2$ on a plant with two lags. The loop is then
\begin{equation}
L(s)=\frac{k\,e^{-sL_d}}{sL_d},\qquad k=\frac{KK_cL_d}{T_i}.
\label{eq:loop}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Exact robustness]
\label{prop:exact}
The loop \eqref{eq:loop} is stable for $0<k<\pi/2$. Its gain margin is
$\pi/(2k)$, and its poles are $W_j(-k)/L_d$, where $W_j$ are the branches
of the Lambert $W$ function \cite{AslUlsoy2003}. Its peak $M_s$ is a
strictly increasing function of $k$, given by
\begin{equation}
k=x\sin x-x^2\cos x,\qquad
\frac{1}{M_s^2}=1-\frac{2k\sin x}{x}+\frac{k^2}{x^2},
\label{eq:param}
\end{equation}
where $x=\omega_pL_d\in(0,\pi/2)$ is the normalized peak frequency.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The phase of $L$ is $-\pi/2-x$. It first reaches $-\pi$ at $x=\pi/2$,
where $|L|=2k/\pi<1$; since $|L|=k/x$ decreases, $|L|<1$ at every later
crossing of $-\pi$, so the loop is stable (Nyquist). The poles solve $sL_de^{sL_d}=-k$. Let
$h(x)=|1+L|^2=1-2k\sin x/x+k^2/x^2$. Then
$h'(x)=2k(\varphi(x)-k)/x^3$, where $\varphi(x)=x\sin x-x^2\cos x$
increases on $(0,\pi)$ and $\varphi(\pi/2)=\pi/2>k$. Hence, on $(0,\pi)$,
$h$ has a single minimum $x^*\in(0,\pi/2)$. On $[\pi,2\pi]$, $h\ge1$;
for $x\ge2\pi$, $h\ge(1-k/x)^2>(1-2k/\pi)^2=h(\pi/2)$. So $x^*$ is the
global minimum, which gives \eqref{eq:param}. By the envelope theorem,
$\frac{d}{dk}h(x^*)=\frac{2}{x^{*2}}(k-x^*\sin x^*)=-2\cos x^*<0$.
\end{proof}

SIMC with $\tau_c=L_d$ and $T\le8L_d$ cancels the lag and gives
$k=\frac12$. Hence the gain margin is $\pi$ and, by \eqref{eq:param}, $M_s=1.59$, which
are exactly the values in \cite[Table~2]{SkogestadGrimholt2012}.

After a unit setpoint step, $e=1$ on $[0,L_d]$ and
$\dot e(t)=-(k/L_d)\,e(t-L_d)$. Let $c_1<c_2<\dots$ be the setpoint
crossings, let $c_0=0$, let lobe $i$ be the interval $[c_i,c_{i+1}]$, and
let $E_0=1$.

\begin{theorem}[Counts on lag-cancelled loops]
\label{thm:counts}
For the loop \eqref{eq:loop} with $0<k<\pi/2$, the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] The error never crosses the setpoint if and only if
$k\le1/\mathrm{e}$, that is, if and only if $M_s\le M_s(1/\mathrm{e})=1.394$. For $k>1/\mathrm{e}$,
it crosses infinitely often.
\item[(b)] Lobe $i$ contains exactly one extremum $E_i$, at $c_i+L_d$.
\item[(c)] $|E_{i+1}|\le r(k)\,|E_i|$, where $r(k)=k^2/2$ for $k\le1$
and $r(k)=k-\frac12$ for $k\ge1$.
\item[(d)] For $k<3/2$, the lobe areas decrease strictly, so
Corollary~\ref{cor:count} holds for $\Gamma_0$: $N_0$ is within half a
turn of half the number of crossings.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(a) The record is the fundamental solution of the delay equation. It is
positive if and only if $s+(k/L_d)e^{-sL_d}=0$ has a real root, that is,
if and only if $k\le1/\mathrm{e}$; for $k>1/\mathrm{e}$, all solutions oscillate
\cite{GyoriLadas1991}. The value of $M_s$ follows from
Proposition~\ref{prop:exact}. (b) The sign of $\dot e(t)$ is that of
$-e(t-L_d)$. By induction over the lobes, $e$ moves away from zero on
$(c_i,c_i+L_d)$, where $e(t-L_d)$ lies in lobe $i-1$; hence lobe $i$ is
longer than $L_d$. On $(c_i+L_d,c_{i+1}+L_d)$, $e$ moves toward zero.
For $i=0$, the extremum is the plateau $e=1$ on $[0,L_d]$. (c) On lobe $i$, $|e|\le|E_i|$. After the extremum,
$|\dot e|\le(k/L_d)|E_i|$, so $e$ needs at least $L_d/k$ to reach zero,
and $|e(t)|\le|E_i|\min\{1,k(c_{i+1}-t)/L_d\}$ on the last $L_d$ of the
lobe (for $t\ge c_i+L_d$ by integrating the bound on $|\dot e|$; for
earlier $t$, $k(c_{i+1}-t)/L_d>1$). Since $e(c_{i+1})=0$,
$E_{i+1}=-\frac{k}{L_d}\int_{c_{i+1}-L_d}^{c_{i+1}}e\,dt$, and integrating
the bound gives $r(k)|E_i|$. (d) Integrating $\dot e$ from $c_i+L_d$ to
$c_{i+1}+L_d$ gives the area $\frac{L_d}{k}(|E_i|+|E_{i+1}|)$ of lobe
$i$, because the extrema alternate in sign. By (c), $|E_{i+2}|<|E_i|$ if
$r(k)<1$, that is, if $k<3/2$, where $M_s=26.2$.
\end{proof}

How many of the crossings a count sees depends on $\delta$; the limit
$\delta\to0$ removes this dependence.

\begin{proposition}[Counts at small $\delta$]
\label{prop:delta}
For the loop \eqref{eq:loop}, let $N(\delta)$ be any of the counts
$N_0,N_1,N_2$, in any linear coordinates with a positive determinant,
which may depend on $\delta$. If $k\le1/\mathrm{e}$, $N(\delta)$ stays
bounded as $\delta\to0$. If $1/\mathrm{e}<k<\pi/2$, let
$\sigma+j\omega=W_0(-k)/L_d$, $\omega>0$. Then
\begin{equation}
N(\delta)=\frac{\omega}{2\pi|\sigma|}\ln\frac1\delta+O(1)
=\frac{\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}}{2\pi\zeta}\ln\frac1\delta+O(1),
\label{eq:rate}
\end{equation}
where $\zeta=|\sigma|/|\sigma+j\omega|$ is the damping ratio of the
dominant poles.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The roots of $sL_de^{sL_d}=-k$ are $W_i(-k)/L_d$; $W_0$ gives the
rightmost, real for $k\le1/\mathrm{e}$ and a simple pair $s,\bar s$
otherwise \cite{ShinozakiMori2006}. The residue of
$\mathcal L\{e\}=L_d/(sL_d+ke^{-sL_d})$ at $s$ is $1/(1+sL_d)\neq0$, so
$e=\mathrm{Re}(ce^{st})+O(e^{\sigma't})$, $c\neq0$, $\sigma'<\sigma$
\cite{BellmanCooke1963}. In each portrait, $q$ is the running sum of $p$
and tends to $0$, so $(p,q)\approx(\mathrm{Re}(sw),\mathrm{Re}\,w)$,
$w=cs^me^{st}$, $m\in\{-1,0,1\}$: the image of $w$, which turns at the
rate $\omega$, under a map with determinant $\omega>0$. A map with a
positive determinant changes a swept angle by less than $\pi$, so the
angle at $t$ is $\omega t+O(1)$. The count stops when $|e|$ last exceeds
$\delta$, at $t_\delta=\ln(1/\delta)/|\sigma|+O(1)$, since $|e|\le
Ce^{\sigma t}$ and $|e|\ge\frac{|c|}{2}e^{\sigma t}$ once per half
period; trimming moves $\Gamma_0$ by $O(\delta)$, which affects only the
last $O(1)$ of the count. For $k\le1/\mathrm{e}$, the leading term has a
fixed direction, so the angle converges.
\end{proof}

So a limit $\bar N$ at a fixed $\delta$ limits the damping of the
dominant poles; the rate \eqref{eq:rate} is zero up to $M_s=1.394$ and
grows with $M_s$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:stair}(a)).

\paragraph*{Robust limits at $\delta=0.02$} Each crossing above
$\delta$ adds half a turn (Lemma~\ref{lem:half}), so the counts form a
staircase. On the loop \eqref{eq:loop}, with $n$ crossings above $\delta$,
all three computed counts lie strictly between $n/2$ and $(n+1)/2$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:stair}(a), right axis). A half-integer limit
$\bar N=(n+1)/2$ thus means exactly ``at most $n$ crossings above
$\delta$'', that is, $M_s\le1.534$, $1.842$, $2.210$ for $n=0,1,2$, where
the $(n+1)$th extremum equals $\delta$. Any other limit lies on a step,
and its meaning depends on the portrait: $\bar N=0.75$ trips at
$M_s=1.54$, $1.57$ or $1.59$. On general loops the staircase breaks: for the
plants of Section~\ref{sec:sim} with random gains, a count with $m$ sign
changes of $p$ left $(m/2,(m+1)/2)$ in $24$, $9$ and $68\%$ of $941$
records ($\Gamma_0,\Gamma_1,\Gamma_2$).

\subsection{When they do not}

Elsewhere, crossings and $M_s$ are not tied. A first-order plant with
$T=0.3L_d$ under PI with $T_i=8T$ and a gain margin of $1.624$ has
$M_s=2.62$, yet its error never crosses the setpoint by more than $2\%$:
the oscillation rides on a slow decay (Fig.~\ref{fig:record}).
Conversely, a controller zero can cause crossings at a low $M_s$. So the
counts can shape a tuning, but they do not certify it; a certificate
must, and the same step contains one.

\section{One Step Reveals $M_s$}
\label{sec:record}

The counts cannot certify $M_s$, but the record they are computed from
can. A digital controller runs with the sampling period $T_s$. Seen at the
samples, the loop it closes is linear and time invariant. From here on,
$L(z)$, $P(z)$, $S=1/(1+L)$ and
$M_s=\max_{\theta\in[0,\pi]}|S(e^{j\theta})|$, with $\theta=\omega T_s$,
refer to this digital loop, the loop that actually runs.

\begin{lemma}[Exact identity]
\label{lem:record}
Let the loop be stable, have one degree of freedom, and have a strictly
proper $L$ (the measurement precedes the control action). Let $e_n$ be
the error after a unit setpoint step at $n=0$, applied from rest and
without disturbances, so that $e_{-1}=0$ and $e_0=1$, and let
$a_n=\Delta e_n=e_n-e_{n-1}$. Then
\begin{equation}
S(e^{j\theta})=\sum_{n\ge0}a_n\,e^{-jn\theta}.
\label{eq:srecord}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The $z$-transform of $e$ is $S(z)/(1-z^{-1})$, so $S=(1-z^{-1})\mathcal Z\{e\}$. In a stable
loop, $a_n$ decays exponentially, so the series converges on the unit
circle.
\end{proof}

\begin{assumption}[Long enough record]
\label{ass:tail}
The loop is stable, and the record $e_0,\dots,e_N$ satisfies
$\sum_{n>N}|a_n|\le\tau$ for a known $\tau$.
\end{assumption}

In a stable loop, $|a_n|\le c\rho^n$ with $\rho<1$, so $\tau$ falls
geometrically with the record length.

\begin{theorem}[Certified bounds from one record]
\label{thm:cert}
Let the conditions of Lemma~\ref{lem:record} and
Assumption~\ref{ass:tail} hold, and let
$\hat S(\theta)=\sum_{n=0}^{N}a_ne^{-jn\theta}$. Let $G$ be a uniform
grid on $[0,\bar\theta]$, $\bar\theta\le\pi$, that has spacing $h$ and
contains both ends. For any $n_c$, let
\begin{align*}
J&=\textstyle\sum_{n=0}^N|n-n_c|\,|a_n|,\;\;
B=\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}|a_{n+1}-a_n|+|a_N|+2\tau.
\end{align*}
Then $\underline M\le M_s\le\overline M$, where
\begin{align}
\underline M&=\textstyle\max_{\theta\in G}|\hat S(\theta)|-\tau,\nonumber\\
\overline M&=\max\Big\{\textstyle\max_{\theta\in G}|\hat S(\theta)|+\frac{hJ}{2}+\tau,\;
1+\frac{B}{\sin(\bar\theta/2)}\Big\},
\label{eq:bound}
\end{align}
and the second term is omitted if $\bar\theta=\pi$. If $\tau$ equals the
true remaining variation, $N\to\infty$, $h\to0$ and $\bar\theta=\pi$,
then both bounds converge to $M_s$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:srecord}, $|S-\hat S|\le\sum_{n>N}|a_n|\le\tau$ at every
$\theta$, which gives $\underline M$. On $[0,\bar\theta]$, $|\hat S|$
equals $|e^{jn_c\theta}\hat S|$, whose derivative is bounded by $J$.
Every $\theta$ lies within $h/2$ of a grid point, which gives the first
term of $\overline M$. On $[\bar\theta,\pi]$, let $z=e^{-j\theta}$ and
$Z_n=\sum_{m=1}^nz^m$, so that $|Z_n|\le2/|1-z|=1/\sin(\theta/2)$.
Summation by parts gives $\sum_{n\ge1}a_nz^n=\sum_{n\ge1}(a_n-a_{n+1})Z_n$.
Since $\sum_{n\ge N}|a_n-a_{n+1}|\le|a_N|+2\tau$, we obtain
$|S-a_0|\le B/\sin(\theta/2)$ with $a_0=1$. Convergence follows because, for fixed $n_c$, $J$ stays bounded as
$N\to\infty$, so $hJ\to0$, and the remaining variation tends to zero.
\end{proof}

All of \eqref{eq:bound} but $\tau$ comes from the record, the grid
values from one chirp $z$-transform; $B$ bounds $[\bar\theta,\pi]$ at once.

\section{Certified \spin}
\label{sec:spin}

\emph{Certified \spin is \spin with one more test: a tuning that the
counts accept is accepted only if the upper bound $\overline M$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}, computed from the same record, is at most a
target $M^*$.} In Algorithm~1, it replaces line 7 by the bold lines
7a--7c; everything else is unchanged. The counts shape the tuning; the
certificate decides whether it is accepted. When the bound rejects a
tuning, certified \spin lowers the gains in one of two ways:
\begin{enumerate*}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item a band move, as in line 10, or
\item a uniform back-off, the reverse of an acceptance.
\end{enumerate*}

\begin{theorem}[Certified \spin]
\label{thm:spin}
Let the plant be stable, let $K_iP(1)>0$, and let every record tested in
line 7a meet the conditions of Lemma~\ref{lem:record} and
Assumption~\ref{ass:tail}. For (iii) and (iv), also let no gain be
clipped by the gain box, and let $\beta<\frac12$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] Every tuning that passes the test of line 7b, and hence the
result of certified \spin, has $M_s\le M^*$.
\item[(ii)] For a controller $C$, let
$\mu=\min\{0,\inf_{0<\theta\le\pi}\mathrm{Re}\,L(e^{j\theta})\}$. For
$0<\lambda<1/|\mu|$, the loop with the controller $\lambda C$ is stable,
and
\begin{equation}
M_s(\lambda C)\le\frac{1}{1-\lambda|\mu|}.
\label{eq:backoff}
\end{equation}
\item[(iii)] If $M^*>1+2\tau+\eta$, every run of consecutive rejections
by the test of line 7b ends, either in an acceptance or in a move of
Algorithm~1 (line 3 or line 10). The run contains at most
$n_{\max}$ band moves, and each stretch of uniform back-offs in it has at
most $\lceil\ln\frac{|\mu|M'}{M'-1}/\ln\frac1{1-\beta}\rceil$ of them,
where $M'=M^*-2\tau-\eta$ and $\mu$ belongs to the controller at the
start of the stretch.
\item[(iv)] Every move except an acceptance lowers
$\Phi=\ln K_i+2\ln K_p+4\ln K_d$ (without the last term for PI) by at
least $\ln\frac1{1-\beta}$. Hence
the gains never return to earlier values without an acceptance in
between.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) This follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}. (ii) $L$ is bounded on the
unit circle except near $z=1$. There, the integral term has a bounded
real part, because $P$ is analytic at $z=1$ and
$\mathrm{Re}\,1/(e^{j\theta}-1)=-\frac12$; so $\mu$ is finite. Then
$|1+\lambda L|\ge1+\lambda\,\mathrm{Re}\,L\ge1-\lambda|\mu|>0$, which gives
\eqref{eq:backoff}. The Nyquist curve of $\lambda L$ lies in
$\mathrm{Re}\ge-\lambda|\mu|>-1$, also on a small enough indentation
outside $z=1$, where $K_iP(1)>0$ keeps $\mathrm{Re}\,L>\mu$. It therefore cannot
encircle $-1$, and since $L$ has no poles outside the unit disc, the loop
is stable. (iii) Line 7c allows at most $n_{\max}$ band moves. In a
stretch of uniform back-offs from a controller $C$, $\lambda=(1-\beta)^j$ after
$j$ back-offs, and \eqref{eq:backoff} gives $M_s\le M'$ for the stated
$j$. In \eqref{eq:bound}, the grid term is then at most
$M_s+\tau+\eta+\tau\le M^*$, and the second term is at most $M^*$ by the
choice of $\bar\theta$; so the test passes unless the counts reject
first. (iv) With $w=(1,2,4)$, a band move changes $\Phi$ by
$\ln\frac1{1-\beta}(\sum_{j<k}w_j-w_k)\le-\ln\frac1{1-\beta}$. A uniform
back-off changes it by $-7\ln\frac1{1-\beta}$, and line 3 by
$-17\ln2<-\ln\frac1{1-\beta}$ for $\beta<\frac12$.
Returning to the same gains requires $\sum\Delta\Phi=0$, hence an
acceptance, which raises $\Phi$.
\end{proof}

So the test never traps \spin. A back-off is safe by (ii) but slows
load rejection; a band move is often cheaper, and the record predicts it.

\begin{proposition}[Prediction from one step]
\label{prop:shape}
For any controller $C'$ on the same plant,
$S'=S/(S+(1-S)C'/C)$ exactly. If the record gives
$\hat S=S+\varepsilon$, the predicted $\hat S'$ has the error
$\hat S'-S'=\varepsilon\,(C'/C)/(D\hat D)$, where $D=S+(1-S)C'/C$ and
$\hat D$ is $D$ with $\hat S$, wherever $D,\hat D\neq0$. The formula
predicts the frequency response of the new loop, not its stability.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$L'=LC'/C$ with $L=(1-S)/S$; subtracting the two expressions gives the
error.
\end{proof}

\section{Simulations}
\label{sec:sim}

\paragraph*{Setup} We compare published and certified \spin. Both run $200$ iterations with a derivative filter ratio
of $10$ and the gain box of \cite{SPIN}. Since the box ties the result to the
start, every loop runs from SIMC on a half-rule model with $\tau_c$ three
times its dead time (cautious start) and from the same tuning with all
gains doubled (bold start). The $19$ test loops are (1) the four plants of \cite{SPIN},
$P_1=e^{-s}/((10s+1)(s+1)^3)$, $P_2=1.25e^{-8s}/(5s+1)^4$,
$P_3=e^{-10s}/((2s+1)(s+1)^3)$ and $P_4=e^{-4s}/(8s+1)^6$, with PI and
with PID; (2) four plants that place the oscillation in a chosen band:
the delay-dominant $e^{-5s}/(s+1)$ and the lag-dominant $e^{-s}/(10s+1)$
and $e^{-0.5s}/((10s+1)(s+1))$ with PI, and the fast
$e^{-0.1s}/((s+1)(0.5s+1))$ with PID; (3) the family
$e^{-L_ds}/((s+1)(0.5s+1)(0.25s+1))$ with PID and
$L_d\in\{0.25,0.5,1,\dots,16\}$ (dead-time share $0.125$ to $0.90$).
P, I and D act on the error. Let $T_a$ be the dead time plus the
lag time constants of the plant and $T_f$ the derivative filter time
constant; the controller runs at $T_s=\min(T_a/100,T_f/20)$ for PID and
$T_a/1000$ for PI, with exactly discretized lags and the delay rounded to
whole samples. Each record covers $15\,T_a$. All values of $M_s$ are
those of this digital loop, computed from its exact $L$. Because \spin ends
in a cycle between a few tunings, we report the most aggressive tuning
accepted in the last $60$ iterations.

\paragraph*{Counts (Section~\ref{sec:count})} On the lag-cancelled loop,
for $126$ values of $k\in(1/\mathrm{e},3/2)$, Theorem~\ref{thm:counts}(c),
(d) and Lemma~\ref{lem:half} held (half turns within $10^{-6}$), and
\eqref{eq:param} matched a direct search to six digits. For
$\delta\in[10^{-12},10^{-3}]$, the counts grew with the slope
\eqref{eq:rate} within $3.3\%$ (remainder ${<}\,0.45$ turn), and for
$k\le1/\mathrm{e}$ varied by ${<}\,0.03$.

\section{Conclusion}

The setpoint step that \spin records in every iteration already contains
$M_s$. (1) Counts cannot certify: they measure crossings and, at small
$\delta$, damping; at $\delta=0.02$ the robust limits are the
half-integers, which bound $M_s$ only on special loops
(Theorem~\ref{thm:counts}, Proposition~\ref{prop:delta}). (2) One step reveals $M_s$: in a digital
loop, one finite record certifies $M_s$ without a model
(Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}); so does every logged setpoint change made from
rest. (3) Certified \spin, with this certificate as its acceptance test,
accepts only tunings with $M_s\le M^*$ and is never trapped
(Theorem~\ref{thm:spin}). Not the counts certify a tuning, but the
certificate. The theorems assume a linear loop and a long enough record; noise and real
loops are left to a follow-up. Scripts: \url{https://github.com/ottapav/robopid-simulator}
(\texttt{docs/ECC27\_certified\_SPIN}).
